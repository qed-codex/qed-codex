# Wang sequence on C × Bℕ, lines, and root systems of units via periodic sequences

Write Bℕ for the one-object category of the monoid ℕ. A functor on C × Bℕ (`New.Variety.TimesBNat`)
is a functor Y on C with an endomorphism γ. Its cohomology plays the part of that of the homotopy
quotient by γ. Cohomology is Hⁿ(Y, F) = Extⁿ(ℤ[Y], F) in functors to abelian groups. Y is a line
over a functor U on C (`New.Variety.IsLine`) if a map from Y to U is bijective on cohomology. Root
systems of a unit are defined on O_C-algebras, twisted by the character s ↦ (ε^s)^♯. For a unit `v`
of a `p`-adic test (`New.Variety.PadicTest`: its ring `ProdRing` is a product of cells `Cell j`,
`TiltRing` is its tilt, and `algFunctor` sends it to `ProdRing` as an O_C-algebra), they form a line
for the coefficients `tilt ⋙ moduleCoeff (tensorCoeff M)`, provided `moduleCoeff (tensorCoeff M)` is
acyclic on perfect rings (`New.Variety.IsAcyclicOnPerfect`). This is proved by representing them,
through tilting, by the ring of periodic sequences. There the twist becomes the shift.

## Main definitions and results

* `New.Variety.TimesBNat.shortExact_wangComplex`: the short exact sequence
  0 → ℤ[Y × ℕ] → ℤ[Y × ℕ] → ℤ[Y] → 0 (`New.Variety.TimesBNat.wangComplex`;
  `New.Variety.TimesBNat.shortExact_induced` is the same with the two maps written out). Its long
  exact sequence is the Wang sequence, since Y × ℕ on C × Bℕ has the cohomology of Y on C
  (`New.Variety.TimesBNat.isLine_fst`).
* `New.Variety.IsLine.of_acyclic`: a criterion for Y to be a line over a representable functor
  Hom(A, −), in terms of the kernel and cokernel of γ - 1 on coefficients
  (`New.Variety.KoszulExact`).
* `New.Variety.compatibleRoots`: A ↦ characters ℚ≥0 → A, that is, elements with a compatible system
  of n-th roots for all n ≥ 1.
* `New.Variety.CompatibleRootsAreLines`: the property of coefficients F and a functor ψ to
  O_C-algebras that the root systems of every unit of every ψ(A) form a line (`New.Variety.IsLine`)
  over Hom(A, −).
* `New.Variety.periodicSeq.koszulExact_shift`: in characteristic `p`, the constants contain the
  kernel of the shift minus the identity, and this map is onto (`New.Variety.KoszulExact`). This
  also holds after tensoring with a module.
* `New.Variety.PadicTest.isLine_compatibleRoots`: if `moduleCoeff (tensorCoeff M)` is acyclic on
  perfect rings (`New.Variety.IsAcyclicOnPerfect`), root systems of `v` have flat base change
  (`New.Variety.HasFlatBaseChange`) and form a line (`New.Variety.IsLine`) for the coefficients
  `tilt ⋙ moduleCoeff (tensorCoeff M)`.

Notions from other files:
* `New.Variety.freeAb`: The functor sending a set-valued functor Y to ℤ[Y], whose value at A is the
  free abelian group on Y(A).
* `New.OC`: The ring of integers O_C of the field C_p of p-adic complex numbers.
* `New.Period.OFlat`: The ring O^♭, the inverse limit of O_C/p along x ↦ x^p, where O_C is the ring
  of integers of C_p.

## Relation to the literature

Known result: Faltings' toric towers and their Koszul complexes. Here the variables are treated one
at a time, and the discrete monoid generated by one automorphism replaces continuous cohomology of
Z_p^d, so that Wang sequences replace the Koszul complex. Source: P. Scholze, p-adic Hodge theory
for rigid-analytic varieties, Forum Math. Pi 1 (2013), Lemmas 4.5 and 5.5; Bhatt, Morrow, Scholze
2018, Lemma 7.3; Stacks project, tag 0EBS; G. Faltings, p-adic Hodge theory, J. Amer. Math. Soc. 1
(1988).



## Homotopy quotients on C × Bℕ, the Wang sequence and lines

`IsBijective (.restrict φ F) (freeAbMap u)` says that Hⁿ(Y, F) → Hⁿ(Y', F ∘ φ) along u : Y' → Y ∘ φ
is bijective for all n.


### `TimesBNat`

 For a category C, the product category C × Bℕ, where Bℕ is the one-object category whose
endomorphism monoid is the additive natural numbers. 

### `TimesBNat.fst`

 The projection functor from the product category C × Bℕ to its first factor C. 

### `TimesBNat.incl`

 The functor C → C × Bℕ sending an object A to (A, ∗) and a morphism f to (f, identity). 

### `TimesBNat.natWithShift`

 The set-valued functor on C × Bℕ with value ℕ at every object, on which morphisms of C act
trivially and k in Bℕ acts by adding k. 

### `TimesBNat.shiftEnd`

 For a set-valued functor Y on C × Bℕ, the additive character of ℕ sending k to the endomorphism
of the restriction of Y to C given at A by Y(id_A, k). 

 ### Criteria for a bijection on cohomology 

### `IsLine`

 For Y on C × Bℕ, U on C and a map a from Y to U (with ℕ acting trivially on U), the property
that Hⁿ(U, F) → Hⁿ(Y, F) is bijective for all n, the coefficients F being pulled back to C × Bℕ. 

### `TimesBNat.isLine_fst`

 For a set-valued functor X on C, the projection X(A) × ℕ → X(A) induces bijections from
Hⁿ(X, F) to the cohomology of X × ℕ on C × Bℕ, ℕ acting by translation, with coefficients F
pulled back. 

### `wangRetraction`

 The retraction in the Wang sequence at one object, for a map `t` of a set `X` to itself: it
sends `(x, 0)` to `0` and `(x, k + 1)` to its value at `(t x, k)` minus `(x, k)`. 

### `TimesBNat.shiftEnd_naturality`

 The maps `(x, k) ↦ γ^k x` from `Y × ℕ` to `Y`, with `γ = shiftEnd Y 1`, are natural on
C × Bℕ: a map `(f, j)` sends `(x, k)` to `(Y(f, 0) x, k + j)`, and `Y(f, j) ∘ γ^k` is
`γ^(k + j) ∘ Y(f, 0)`. 

### `TimesBNat.shortExact_induced`

 For Y on C × Bℕ with endomorphism γ, the sequence 0 → ℤ[Y × ℕ] → ℤ[Y × ℕ] → ℤ[Y] → 0 is short
exact. Here Y × ℕ is the product of the restriction of Y to C, pulled back to C × Bℕ, with
`New.Variety.TimesBNat.natWithShift`; the first map is (x, k) ↦ (γ x, k) - (x, k + 1), the second is
(x, k) ↦ γ^k x, and `w` is the proof that their composite is zero. 

### `TimesBNat.wangSub`

 The first map of the Wang sequence, ℤ[Y × ℕ] → ℤ[Y × ℕ], `(x, k) ↦ (γ x, k) - (x, k + 1)` with
`γ = shiftEnd Y 1`. Here Y × ℕ is the restriction of Y to C, pulled back to C × Bℕ, times
`New.Variety.TimesBNat.natWithShift`. 

### `TimesBNat.wangPow`

 The second map of the Wang sequence, ℤ[Y × ℕ] → ℤ[Y], `(x, k) ↦ γ^k x` with
`γ = shiftEnd Y 1`. 

### `TimesBNat.wangSub_comp_wangPow`

 The two maps of the Wang sequence compose to zero, as `γ^k (γ x) = γ^(k + 1) x`. 

### `TimesBNat.wangComplex`

 The Wang sequence ℤ[Y × ℕ] → ℤ[Y × ℕ] → ℤ[Y] (`wangSub`, then `wangPow`), a short complex of
functors from C × Bℕ to abelian groups. 

### `TimesBNat.shortExact_wangComplex`

 For Y on C × Bℕ with endomorphism γ, the Wang sequence 0 → ℤ[Y × ℕ] → ℤ[Y × ℕ] → ℤ[Y] → 0 is
short exact (`wangComplex`): the first map is `(x, k) ↦ (γ x, k) - (x, k + 1)`, the second
`(x, k) ↦ γ^k x`. 

### `KoszulExact`

 The map i is injective, every fixed point of g lies in its image, and z ↦ g(z) − z is onto. 

### `IsLine.of_acyclic`

 Let `Y` be a set-valued functor on `C × Bℕ`, let `ψ : C ⥤ C'`, `A : C` and `E : C'`, and let
`F'` be an abelian-group-valued functor on `C'`. Let `e` identify the restriction of `Y` to `C` with
`Hom(E, ψ −)`, let `q : Y → Hom(A, −)` be induced by `i : ψ A → E` (`hq_eq_comp`), and let the
generator of ℕ act on `Y` by `g : E → E` (`h_shift_square`: through `e` it is `reprMap g`,
precomposition with `g` on `reprBy E = Hom(E, −)`). Suppose that restriction along `ψ` is bijective
on the cohomology of `Hom(E, −)` with coefficients `F'` (`New.Variety.IsAcyclicAt`), and that
`F'(i)` and `F'(g)` satisfy `New.Variety.KoszulExact`: `F'(i)` is injective, every fixed point of
`F'(g)` lies in the image of `F'(i)`, and `z ↦ F'(g) z − z` is onto. Then `q` is a line: for every
`n`, the map `Hⁿ(Hom(A, −), F' ∘ ψ) → Hⁿ(Y, F' ∘ ψ)` is bijective. 


## Compatible root systems twisted by ε

`New.Period.epsPow` is the chosen homomorphism s ↦ ε^s from ℚ to the tilt O^♭ of O_C. The symbol ♯
is Mathlib's `PreTilt.untilt`.

* `New.Variety.compatibleRoots.atOne`: the value at 1. The twist does not change it, since ε^♯ = 1.


### `epsPowNNRat`

 The character s ↦ ε^s of ℚ≥0 with values in O^♭, the restriction of the chosen character
`New.Period.epsPow` of ℚ. 

### `underlyingSet.`

 The functor sending a commutative O_C-algebra to its underlying set, lifted to an arbitrary
universe. 

### `compatibleRoots`

 The functor on (O_C-algebras) × Bℕ sending A to the monoid homomorphisms χ from (ℚ≥0, +) to
(A, ·). A map (f, k) from A to B sends χ to η^k · (f ∘ χ), where η is the character s ↦ (ε^s)^♯
read in B. 

### `compatibleRoots.atOne`

 The natural map, on O_C-algebras times Bℕ, sending a character ℚ≥0 → A to its value at 1. 

### `compatibleRootsAt`

 Let ψ be a functor from C to O_C-algebras and y a point of ψ(A). This is the functor on C × Bℕ,
the fibre product (`fibreProd`) of `compatibleRoots.atOne` along ψ with the point `pointMap y`,
sending B to the pairs of a character w : ℚ≥0 → ψ(B) and a map f : A → B with w(1) = ψ(f)(y). Such a
w is a system of roots of the image of y. 

### `CompatibleRootsAreLines`

 For every object A and unit y of ψ(A), the root systems of y (`New.Variety.compatibleRootsAt`)
form a line over Hom(A, −). That is, the projection to Hom(A, −) is bijective on cohomology with
coefficients F. 


## Periodic sequences and root systems of a unit of a test

A root system of a unit `v` of a `p`-adic test (`New.Variety.PadicTest`) is a character of `ℚ≥0`
with value `v` at `1`.

* `New.Variety.periodicSeq.koszulExact_shift`: the constants contain the kernel of the shift minus
  the identity, and this map is onto.
* `New.Variety.periodicSeq.existsUnique_hom`: the universal property, over one domain.
* `New.Variety.PadicTest.isLine_compatibleRoots`: the root systems of a unit have flat base change
  and form a line.


### `periodicSeq`

 The `R`-algebra of periodic sequences `ℕ → R`. A sequence has a positive period if and only
if `n !` is a period for all large `n`; the latter form is the one that is visibly closed under
sums and products. 

### `periodicSeq.shift`

 The shift of periodic sequences, `(γ f) k = f (k + 1)`. 

### `instance {n : ℕ} : NeZero n !`

 The factorial of a natural number is not zero. 

### `periodicSeq.mem_of_periodic`

 A sequence `f` with a positive period `n` is periodic in the sense of `New.Variety.periodicSeq`:
for `m ≥ n` the number `m !` is a multiple of `n`, hence a period. 

### `periodicSeq.koszulExact_shift`

 Let `R` be an `O^♭`-algebra, so of characteristic `p`. Let `γ` be the shift. The inclusion of
the constants in the periodic sequences is injective, and its image contains the kernel of `γ - 1`.
The map `γ - 1` is onto. These facts hold after tensoring with any `O^♭`-module `M`, that is, for
the coefficients `moduleCoeff (tensorCoeff M)`, `B ↦ B ⊗ M`. 

### `periodicSeq.exists_eq_eval`

 For a ring map ψ from the periodic R-valued sequences to a ring without zero divisors and
finitely many sequences w_i, there is one index k with ψ(w_i) = ψ(w_i(k)) for all i, w_i(k)
read as a constant sequence. 

### `periodicSeq.existsUnique_hom`

 The Kummer torsor. Let `ζ`, `r`, `u` be characters of `ℚ≥0` in a domain `E`, with `ζ a` a
primitive root of unity of order the denominator of `a` and `u 1 = r 1 ≠ 0`. Let `D` be a ring
without zero divisors, `f : R →+* D`, let `s : D → E` be any map, and let the periodic sequence
`x n` lift `k ↦ ζ n ^ k * r n` along `s ∘ f`. Then `f` has exactly one extension `ϕ` to periodic
sequences with `s (ϕ (x n)) = u n` for all `n`. 

### `periodicSeq.perfectRing`

 If `R` is perfect of characteristic `p`, so is the ring of periodic sequences over `R`:
Frobenius acts termwise, and a sequence is periodic exactly when its termwise image is. 

### `injective_of_isCell`

 Every ring map `g` from `O_C` to a cell `T` (`New.Variety.IsCell`: a `p`-adically complete,
absolutely integrally closed valuation domain in which `p ≠ 0`) is injective. 

### `PadicTest.baseOfHom`

 The map of tests `c ⟶ D` whose tilt agrees on constant sequences with the `O^♭`-algebra map `ϕ`
from the periodic sequences over the tilt of `c` to the tilt of `D`. 

### `PadicTest.tiltMap_baseOfHom_apply`

 The tilt of `PadicTest.baseOfHom ϕ` is `ϕ` on constant sequences. 

### `PadicTest.baseOfHom_eq_of_forall_algebraMap`

 `PadicTest.baseOfHom ϕ` is the map of tests `f` whose tilt agrees with `ϕ` on constant
sequences. 

### `PadicTest.untilt_map_eq_mul_of_forall`

 A termwise relation between the untilts `♯` of two periodic sequences passes through `ϕ`: if
`(w k)^♯ = t * (w' k)^♯` for every `k`, then `(ϕ w)^♯ = t' * (ϕ w')^♯`, where `t'` is the image of
`t` under `PadicTest.baseOfHom ϕ`. 

### `PadicTest.existsUnique_baseOfHom_eq_and_untilt_eq`

 Let `ζ` be the character `a ↦ (ε ^ a)^♯`, `r` a character of `ℚ≥0` with `r 1` a unit, and let
the periodic sequences `x n` over the tilt of `c` have untilts `k ↦ ζ n ^ k * r n`. For a map of
tests `f : c ⟶ D` and a character `u` in the ring of `D` with `u 1 = f (r 1)`, exactly one `ϕ`
from the periodic sequences to the tilt of `D` has `baseOfHom ϕ = f` and `(ϕ (x n))^♯ = u n`. 

### `PadicTest.exists_rootSystem_periodicSeq`

 Let `v` be a unit of the ring of the test `c`, and `ζ` the character `a ↦ (ε ^ a)^♯` of `ℚ≥0`
in that ring. There are a root system `r` of `v` (a character of `ℚ≥0` with `r 1 = v`) and periodic
sequences `x n` over the tilt of `c` whose untilts are `k ↦ ζ n ^ k * r n`. 

### `PadicTest.isLine_compatibleRoots`

 Let the coefficients `B ↦ B^♭ ⊗ M` make `Hom(b, (-)^♭)` acyclic for every perfect `b`, and let
`v` be a unit of the ring of a test `c`. The root systems of `v` (pairs of a character `u` of `ℚ≥0`
and a map of tests `f : c ⟶ D` with `u 1 = f v`, the generator of `ℕ` multiplying `u` by
`a ↦ (ε ^ a)^♯`) (a) have flat base change and (b) form a line over the functor represented by `c`:
the projection `(u, f) ↦ f` identifies the cohomology of the functor represented by `c` with that
of the homotopy quotient.
