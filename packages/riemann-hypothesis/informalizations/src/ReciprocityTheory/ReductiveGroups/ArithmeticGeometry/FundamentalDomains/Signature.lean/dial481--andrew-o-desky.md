## Complex uniformizations and period lattices

[Definition / hypothesis AG.D140](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/ReciprocityTheory.ChartRestriction.G.FD.Data/)

Assume ([Polarized modules and unitary moduli](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/ReciprocityTheory.ChartRestriction.G.Geo.Tor.Host/)) and ([Coefficient fields and deeper moduli levels](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/ReciprocityTheory.ChartRestriction.G.Geo.Tor.Level/)), using their notation $E,\rho,\rho_\infty,\Psi,K_0,K,X,M_2,\mathfrak b_2,A_2$ and stage signature $(p_{\rm sig},q_{\rm sig})$. Put $r=p_{\rm sig}+q_{\rm sig}$.

Hypothesis (complex uniformizations). For every embedding $\sigma:K\hookrightarrow\mathbb C$ fixing $\rho_\infty$, the following conditions hold. There is a finite collection of Hermitian lattice domains $\mathcal D_c$, each with a map $u_c$ to framed points of $X_\sigma=X\times_{K,\sigma}\mathbb C$. Each domain has a nondegenerate Hermitian matrix $G_c$ on $E^r$, of signature $(q_{\rm sig},p_{\rm sig})$ at every embedding in $\Psi$, and a finitely generated $\mathcal O_E$-lattice $\Lambda_c\subset E^r$ spanning $E^r$. There is $b_c\in\mathcal O_E\setminus\{0\}$ such that $b_ch_c(v,w)$ is integral for $v,w\in\Lambda_c$, where $$h_c(v,w)=\sum_{i,j}v_i(G_c)_{ij}\bar w_j.$$ For a collection of matrices $x=(x_\tau)_{\tau\in\Psi}$, let $\Delta_{\tau,i}(x)$ be the determinant of the Hermitian Gram matrix of its first $i$ columns, with $\Delta_{\tau,0}=1$. The domain consists exactly of invertible frames with $$(-1)^{\max(0,i-q_{\rm sig})}\Re\Delta_{\tau,i}(x)>0\qquad(\tau\in\Psi,\ 0\le i\le r).$$ Its triangular group consists of all invertible upper triangular matrices at these embeddings. There is a subgroup $\Gamma_c$ of the $E$-linear isometries preserving $\Lambda_c$, of finite index in that isometry group. If $\gamma x=xb$ with $x\in\mathcal D_c$, $\gamma\in\Gamma_c$ and $b$ in the triangular group, then $\gamma=1$.

Each domain has a nonzero $\xi_c\in E$ with $\bar\xi_c=-\xi_c$, an element $\kappa_c\in\{2\xi_c,-2\xi_c\}$ with $\Im\tau(\kappa_c)<0$ for every $\tau\in\Psi$, and an integer $m_c>0$. Whenever $v\in\Lambda_c$ is isotropic and $a\in\mathcal O_E$ is real, $$\left(\delta_{ij}+m_ca\kappa_cv_i\sum_k(G_c)_{jk}\bar v_k\right)_{ij}\in\Gamma_c.$$ Here $\delta_{ij}$ is the Kronecker delta. For each integral domain weight $\nu=(\nu_{\tau,i})$, choose a function $\chi_{c,\nu}:\mathrm{GL}_r(E)\to\mathbb C$ whose restriction to $\Gamma_c$ is a finite-order character, and require $\chi_{c,\nu+\nu'}=\chi_{c,\nu}\chi_{c,\nu'}$ on $\Gamma_c$. Values off $\Gamma_c$ are arbitrary. An automorphic holomorphic section $\Phi$ satisfies $$\Phi(xb)=\prod_{\tau,i}b_{\tau,ii}^{\nu_{\tau,i}}\Phi(x),\qquad \Phi(\gamma x)=\chi_{c,\nu}(\gamma)\Phi(x).$$ The cusp condition additionally requires boundedness of $$|\Phi(x)|\prod_{\tau\in\Psi}\prod_{i=0}^{r-1}\left|\frac{\Delta_{\tau,i+1}(x)}{\Delta_{\tau,i}(x)}\right|^{-\nu_{\tau,i}/2}$$ and vanishing of the constant Fourier–Jacobi coefficient at every nonzero isotropic vector $v\in\Lambda_c$. This coefficient is the normalized average of $\Phi(T_v(s)x)$ in the real parameters $s=(s_\tau)_{\tau\in\Psi}$, where $$T_v(s)_\tau=\left(\delta_{ij}-2\mathrm i|\tau(\xi_c)|s_\tau\tau(v_i)\sum_k\tau((G_c)_{jk})\overline{\tau(v_k)}\right)_{ij}.$$ The average is over a measurable fundamental domain of the closure of $\{(m_cn_{c,\nu}\tau(a))_\tau:a\in\mathcal O_E,\ \bar a=a\}$, with $n_{c,\nu}>0$ an order annihilating $\chi_{c,\nu}$; the coefficient is defined to be zero if no such domain has positive finite volume.

For the original moduli family, choose such Hermitian-lattice domain data at every natural stage $j$ and every embedding $\sigma:K_0\hookrightarrow\mathbb C$, with a finite index set at each $(j,\sigma)$. Each original domain has its own CM type $\Psi_c$, containing exactly one of $\tau,\bar\tau$ for each embedding, and an integer $p_c\in\{0,\ldots,r\}$. In the domain, lattice, group, character and transvection conditions just given, replace $\Psi$ by $\Psi_c$ and the signature $(q_{\rm sig},p_{\rm sig})$ by $(p_c,r-p_c)$. Choose a map from each original domain to framed points of the original stage-$j$ moduli scheme. The uniformization conditions in the remaining paragraphs concern the deeper moduli scheme at the chosen stage.

Every framed point is obtained from some $u_c(x)$ by a triangular change of frame. Two such points are related by a triangular change of frame if and only if they have the same label $c$ and their frames differ by an element of $\Gamma_c$ followed by a triangular change.

The weight hypotheses quantify over arrays $a_{\tau,i}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0}$, for every embedding $\tau:E\hookrightarrow\mathbb C$ and integer $i\ge0$, nonincreasing in $i$, and even integers $n_{\rm wt}\ge0$. For each $\tau$, let $\tau_K:E\hookrightarrow K$ be its lift through $K\subset\mathbb C$, put $\rho_\sigma(\tau)=\sigma\circ\tau_K$, and write $a^\sigma_{\tau,i}=a_{\rho_\sigma^{-1}(\tau),i}$. The associated domain weight, for $\tau\in\Psi$ and $0\le i<r$, is $$\lambda^U_{\tau,i}=\begin{cases} -a^\sigma_{\bar\tau,q_{\rm sig}-1-i}-n_{\rm wt}/2,&0\le i<q_{\rm sig},\\ a^\sigma_{\tau,i-q_{\rm sig}}+n_{\rm wt}/2,&q_{\rm sig}\le i<r. \end{cases}$$ Pullback of every algebraic section with graded-frame exponents $a$ and scalar exponent $n_{\rm wt}$ gives an automorphic section of weight $\lambda^U$ on each $\mathcal D_c$, and the weight-$\lambda^U$ automorphy character is trivial on $\Gamma_c$. Each $u_c(x)$ is induced by extension of scalars from a framed point over a ring $K[z_1,\ldots,z_s]/\mathfrak j$ with $\mathfrak j$ radical, for some $s\ge0$ and a $K$-algebra map from this ring to $\mathbb C$, where $K$ acts through $\sigma$.

An algebraic section is a natural evaluation rule on framed points over reduced finite-type coefficient algebras, transforming under triangular changes by the character of its weight line bundle. At a framed point over an arbitrary coefficient algebra, evaluate by scalar extension from a chosen framed point over a reduced finite-type coefficient algebra when such a presentation exists, and set the value to zero otherwise. For the arbitrary integral weight $\lambda$ in the theorem, its induced domain weight is $$\nu^\sigma_{\tau,i}=\begin{cases} \lambda_{\rho_\sigma^{-1}(\tau),p_{\rm sig}+i},&0\le i<q_{\rm sig},\\ \lambda_{\rho_\sigma^{-1}(\tau),i-q_{\rm sig}},&q_{\rm sig}\le i<r. \end{cases}$$

Put $g=\sum_\tau m_\tau$ and give $\mathbb C^g$ coordinates $(\tau,i)$, $0\le i<m_\tau$, with $a\in\mathcal O_E$ acting in coordinate $(\tau,i)$ through $\rho_\sigma(\tau)(a)$. A period map from an integral polarized module $\Lambda'$ of this signature means an $\mathcal O_E$-equivariant injective additive map $P:\Lambda'\to\mathbb C^g$ with discrete image spanning $\mathbb C^g$ over $\mathbb R$, together with existence of a positive Hermitian form whose imaginary part on periods is $E_{\Lambda'}$.

There is a positive finite family of such integral modules $\Lambda'_b$ and maps $t_b:\Lambda\to\Lambda'_b$ linear over $\mathcal O_E$ and inducing isomorphisms modulo $M_2$. For every additive map $P:\Lambda'_b\to\mathbb C^g$, choose a complex moduli point $v_b(P)$ and a function from $\mathbb C^g$ to its fiber. For a period map $P$, require $v_b(P)\in X_\sigma$ and require that function to be a surjective additive $\mathcal O_E$-equivariant exponential map $$\exp_{b,P}:\mathbb C^g\longrightarrow A_{v_b(P)}(\mathbb C),\qquad \ker\exp_{b,P}=P(\Lambda'_b),$$ where $A_y$ is the abelian variety at $y$. For every positive integer $k$ their Weil pairing at $\exp_{b,P}(P(a)/k)$ and $\exp_{b,P}(P(a')/k)$ is $\exp(-2\pi\mathrm i E_{\Lambda'_b}(a,a')/k)$. The main level point of $a\in\bar{\mathfrak b}_2\Lambda$ is $\exp_{b,P}(P(t_ba)/M_2)$; the auxiliary level point of every $a\in\Lambda$ is $\exp_{b,P}(P(t_ba)/A_2)$.

Every point of $X_\sigma$ occurs as some $v_b(P)$, and equality between two such points requires the same index $b$. For fixed $b$, $v_b(P)=v_b(P')$ if and only if there are a polarization-preserving $\mathcal O_E$-linear automorphism $\gamma$ of $\Lambda'_b$ and a complex-linear isomorphism $h$ of $\mathbb C^g$ with $P'\gamma=hP$, such that $\gamma a-a\in M_2\Lambda'_b$ for $a\in\bar{\mathfrak b}_2\Lambda'_b$ and $\gamma a-a\in A_2\Lambda'_b$ for every $a$. The derivative of each exponential at zero identifies $\mathbb C^g$ bijectively with the tangent space. For every holomorphic family of period maps on an open subset of a finite-dimensional complex vector space, the maps $v_b(P)$ and the exponential maps, jointly in the parameter and vector variables, are holomorphic. Every holomorphic family of points of $X_\sigma$ has local holomorphic period lifts.

On a connected open parameter set, if an integral symplectic basis, up to sign, gives normalized periods $(I_g,\Omega)$ with $\Im\Omega$ positive definite, the polarization has local nowhere-zero holomorphic trivializations, compatible with its transition functions and rigidification, in which a section on each fiber is a Riemann theta function with characteristic coordinates each $0$ or $1/2$, fixed on that set. Here $\Omega$ is the normalized period matrix.

The uniformizations identify the polarized integral homology with the domain lattices. In these identifications the alternating form on a domain lattice $\Lambda_c$ is $$E_c(a,b)=\operatorname{Tr}_{E/\mathbb Q}\{-\xi_c h_c(a,b)\}.$$ Each domain label $c$ selects one $b$ and an isomorphism of polarized $\mathcal O_E$-modules $\Lambda'_b\simeq\Lambda_c$. For $x\in\mathcal D_c$, there is a period map $P_x:\Lambda_c\to\mathbb C^g$ whose quotient is the abelian variety represented by $u_c(x)$. For $\alpha=\rho_\sigma(\tau)$ its coordinate at $(\tau,i)$ is $$P_x(v)_{\tau,i}=\begin{cases} (x_\alpha^{-1}\alpha(v))_{q_{\rm sig}+i},&\alpha\in\Psi,\\ h_{c,\bar\alpha}(\operatorname{col}_{q_{\rm sig}-1-i}x_{\bar\alpha},\bar\alpha(v)),&\alpha\notin\Psi, \end{cases}$$ where $h_{c,\beta}$ is $h_c$ with its coefficients embedded by $\beta$, and $\beta(v)$ means coordinatewise application. The derivatives of its exponential on the coordinate basis are the tangent frame of $u_c(x)$. Whenever all $\Delta_{\tau,i}(x)$ have absolute value one, the polarization has local nowhere-zero holomorphic trivializations $f_a(z)$, a positive Hermitian form $H$ and a function $\chi:\mathbb C^g\to\mathbb C$ with $|\chi(P_xv)|=1$ such that $$\Im H(P_xv,P_xw)=E_c(v,w),$$ $$f_a(z)=\chi(P_xv)\exp\{\pi H(z,P_xv)+\tfrac\pi2H(P_xv,P_xv)\}\,f_a(z+P_xv).$$ These trivializations have the polarization line bundle's transition functions and prescribed values at zero. The generating section in $u_c(x)$ pulls back as $\theta(z)f_a(z)$, and for an additive fundamental domain $D_x$ of the period lattice, $$\frac{\int_{D_x}|\theta(z)|^2\exp\{-\pi\Re H(z,z)\}\,dz}{\operatorname{vol}(D_x)}=1.$$ The indices $a$ label line-bundle trivializations. All minor normalization conditions quantify over every $\tau\in\Psi$ and $0\le i\le r$.
