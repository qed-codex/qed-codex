## Hypothesis list B: fifty-three assumed statements on Weil representations, theta lifts, discrete series, L-functions and complex multiplication

[Definition / hypothesis THETA.D095](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/GlobalTheory.FunPr.ThetaLocalInputs/)

Assume all fifty-three statements below.

**Setting and notation.**

Use the notation and definitions in “Local fields”, “Real unitary groups”, “Number fields” and “The datum $D$ and its unitary groups”, under “Setting and notation” in ([Hypothesis list A: sixty assumed statements on representations of p-adic and real groups, automorphic forms, Galois representations and class field theory](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/GlobalTheory.Fc.S0.UnitaryGroupThirdPlaceHypotheses/)). The datum, its rank and field data, and Hermitian spaces retain the meanings given in ([Identifications for arithmetic family members](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.MainNotions.N.LocTie/)), ([Family fields and Galois data](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.HC.Frame/)) and ([Hermitian spaces in the theta constructions](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.BasicNotions.CMData/)), respectively.

*Lattices.* A *lattice* $L\subset\mathbb{C}$ is given by a pair of periods, and $j(L)$ is the $j$-invariant of a Weierstrass curve attached to $L$ by the project (not expanded). For an imaginary quadratic field $K\subset\mathbb{C}$ and a fractional ideal $\mathfrak{a}$, $j(\mathfrak{a})$ is $j$ of the lattice $\mathfrak{a}\subset\mathbb{C}$.

**The statements.**

**(1)** Assume the squarefree polynomial form of ([Cuspidal characters of finite general linear groups](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/GlobalTheory.greenRegularCharacters_holds/)).

**(2)** *A polynomial bound for the least element of a set attached to a residue class.* There are constants $C,L>0$ such that for all coprime $a,q\ge1$ the least element $p(a,q)$ of a set of natural numbers attached to $(a,q)$ (not expanded) satisfies $p(a,q)\le Cq^L$. If the set is that of the primes congruent to $a$ modulo $q$, this is Linnik's theorem.

**(3)** *A class field of an imaginary quadratic field generated by $j$.* Let $K\subset\mathbb{C}$ be an imaginary quadratic field. There are a subfield $H\subset\mathbb{C}$ containing $K$ and an isomorphism $\mathrm{art}$ from the class group of $K$ to $\mathrm{Aut}(H/K)$ such that: $H$ satisfies a condition and contains every intermediate field of $\mathbb{C}/K$ satisfying it (not expanded); $\mathrm{art}$ sends the class of a nonzero prime $\mathfrak{p}$ to an arithmetic Frobenius at every prime of $H$ over $\mathfrak{p}$; $K(j(\mathfrak{a}))=H$ for every fractional ideal $\mathfrak{a}$; and $\mathrm{art}([\mathfrak{b}])\,j(\mathfrak{a})=j(\mathfrak{b}^{-1}\mathfrak{a})$ for all fractional ideals $\mathfrak{a},\mathfrak{b}$. If the condition is that of being an unramified abelian extension of $K$ and the curve attached to a lattice is $y^2=4x^3-g_2x-g_3$, $H$ is the Hilbert class field and this is the main theorem of complex multiplication for $j$.

**(4)** *Two statements on representations of $\mathrm{GL}_{n+1}$ of a finite field.* For a finite field $\kappa$, call a complex representation of $\mathrm{GL}_{n+1}(\kappa)$ a *$\mathcal{C}'$-representation* if for every nonconstant nondecreasing sequence $b_1\le\dots\le b_{n+1}$ of natural numbers, no nonzero vector is fixed by a subgroup attached to $b$ (not expanded). For every $\kappa$ and $n\ge0$: (i) $\mathrm{GL}_{n+1}(\kappa)$ has a finite-dimensional irreducible $\mathcal{C}'$-representation; (ii) for every nontrivial additive character $\psi_0$ of $\kappa$, the restriction of every finite-dimensional irreducible $\mathcal{C}'$-representation to a fixed subgroup $Q$ is isomorphic to the representation coinduced to $Q$ from a one-dimensional representation, built from $\psi_0$, of a second subgroup (the subgroups and that representation were not expanded). If the subgroups attached to $b$ are the unipotent radicals of the proper standard parabolic subgroups, $Q$ is the mirabolic subgroup, and the one-dimensional representation is the generic character of the unitriangular group, this is Gelfand's theorem on cuspidal representations of finite general linear groups.

**(5)** *A branching statement from size $n+1$ to size $n$.* For every $n\ge1$ and every dominant integral weight $\lambda$ of length $n+1$ in ([Integral forms on column representations](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.BasicNotions.MemberForms/)), a condition holds both for $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{C})$ and for $U(n)$ with its inclusion in $\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{C})$: two representations attached to $\lambda$ are isomorphic, and for every dominant integral weight $\mu$ of length $n$ a number attached to $(\lambda,\mu)$ is $1$ if $\mu$ satisfies a condition relative to $\lambda$ and $0$ otherwise. The representations, the number and the condition were not expanded. If they are the restriction of the irreducible representation of highest weight $\lambda$, the sum of the irreducible representations of interlacing highest weights, the multiplicity, and interlacing, this is Weyl's branching rule.

**(12)** *An identity between a value attached to a zeta-type family and an inner product.* Let $D$ be a datum and $A,B$ Hermitian spaces with $n_A<n_B\le2n_A$ whose Witt index $r_B$ is $0$ or satisfies $n_A+r_B<n_B$. Let $\omega$ be a Weil representation for $(\psi,A,B,\chi_A,\chi_B)$ and $\omega_d$ one for $(\psi,A^\square,B,1,\chi_B)$, satisfying together a condition; let $a$ be adapted to $A$ such that the Iwasawa decomposition holds for $\hat K_a$; fix a Haar measure on $W_{A,B}$ for which a subgroup has a fundamental domain of measure $1$; let the infinity family and the remaining choices be as in part (iii) of item (13); let $m$, $m_B$ be Haar measures on $G_A(\mathbb{A})$, $G_B(\mathbb{A})$, with $m$ right invariant. Let $\mathcal{U}$ be a subspace of a space attached to $(A,m)$, contained in a second subspace, satisfying a condition, and such that a set of functions attached to $\mathcal{U}$ satisfies a further condition relative to $B$ and the characters. Let $\Phi_1,\Phi_2$ be theta functions for $(\psi,A,B)$ such that a function $\delta(\Phi_1,\Phi_2)$ on $W_{A^\square,B}$ satisfies a finiteness condition relative to $\omega_d$ and $\hat K_a$, and let $f_1,f_2$ belong to the set of functions attached to $\mathcal{U}$. Then for $i=1,2$ a convergence condition holds for $(\omega,m,\Phi_i,f_i)$ and a function $\vartheta_i$ on $G_B(\mathbb{A})$ attached to them satisfies a square-integrability condition on $[G_B]$; and a relation holds between the family of functions on $G_{A^\square}(\mathbb{A})$ attached to $\delta(\Phi_1,\Phi_2)$, the character $\chi_B^{-1}$, $f_1$, $f_2$, the number $s_0(A,B)$ and the number $\mathrm{vol}([G_B])^{-1}\langle\vartheta_1,\vartheta_2\rangle$, where $\langle\,,\rangle$ is a pairing attached to $B$ and $m_B$. None of the objects and conditions named here was expanded. If $\mathcal{U}$ is an irreducible cuspidal subrepresentation of $L^2([G_A])$ whose theta lifts to smaller spaces vanish, $\vartheta_i$ the theta lift of $f_i$ with kernel $\Phi_i$, $\langle\,,\rangle$ the Petersson product, and the relation says that the doubling zeta integral has that value at $s_0(A,B)$, this is the Rallis inner product formula.

**(15)** *Hermitian forms: local classification and Hasse principle for CM fields.* (i) Let $K$ be a nonarchimedean local field with $2\ne0$, $L/K$ a quadratic field extension with nontrivial automorphism $\sigma$, and $G,G'$ nondegenerate $\sigma$-Hermitian matrices of size $r$ over $L$. Then $G'=P^{t}G\,\sigma(P)$ for some invertible $P$ if and only if $\det G/\det G'$ is a norm from $L$. (ii) Let $E$ be a CM field and $G,G'$ nondegenerate Hermitian matrices of size $r$ over $E$. If $G$ and $G'$ are equivalent in the same sense over $E^+_v\otimes_{E^+}E$ for every finite prime $v$ of $E^+$, and $\tau(G)$, $\tau(G')$ are equivalent as complex Hermitian matrices for every embedding $\tau$ of $E$, then they are equivalent over $E$. These are the classification of Hermitian forms over local fields by rank and discriminant and Landherr's Hasse principle. The statement is entirely in terms of standard objects.

**(16)** *Class field theory for quadratic extensions.* Let $L/K$ be a quadratic extension of number fields. For a place $v$ of $K$ and $x\in K_v^\times$ put $\eta_v(x)=1$ if $x$ is a norm from $K_v\otimes_KL$ and $\eta_v(x)=-1$ otherwise. Then: (i) every $\eta_v$ is multiplicative; (ii) $t\in K^\times$ is a norm from $L$ if and only if it is a norm at every place; (iii) every $t\in K^\times$ is a norm at almost all finite places, and a family $(x_v)$ of local elements that are norms at almost all finite places differs from some $t\in K^\times$ by local norms everywhere if and only if $\prod_v\eta_v(x_v)=1$, and some such family has product $-1$; (iv) $\prod_v\eta_v(t)=1$ for $t\in K^\times$. Moreover, if $E/F$ is a quadratic extension of number fields, $K/F$ a finite extension and $L=KE$ quadratic over $K$, then for every completion $A$ of $F$ and every finite field extension $B$ of $A$ containing $K$ compatibly, the symbol of $L/K$ on $B^\times$ is the symbol of $E/F$ on $A^\times$ composed with the norm $B^\times\to A^\times$. These are the Hasse norm theorem, the reciprocity law and the compatibility of the norm residue symbol with base change, for quadratic extensions. The statement is entirely in terms of standard objects.

**(17)** *Bijectivity of a map between two spaces of fixed vectors.* For every $k$ and every admissible representation $\pi$ of $\mathrm{GL}_n(k)$, a linear map is bijective: it goes from the vectors of $\pi$ fixed by a subgroup $\mathcal{I}$ to the vectors, fixed by a group $\mathcal{T}$, of a representation built from $\pi$ on a quotient of its space, and is induced by the quotient map. The two groups and the built representation were not expanded. If $\mathcal{I}$ is the Iwahori subgroup, the quotient is the space of coinvariants for the unipotent radical of the Borel subgroup and $\mathcal{T}$ the integral points of the diagonal torus, this is the theorem of Borel and Casselman.

**(18)** *A polynomial algebra on Lyndon words.* Let $K$ be a field of characteristic $0$ and $\mathcal{A}$ a totally ordered set. A nonempty word in $\mathcal{A}$ is a *Lyndon word* if it is lexicographically smaller than each of its proper nonempty suffixes. Let $\mathcal{W}$ be the space of finite $K$-linear combinations of words, with an algebra structure that was not expanded. The algebra homomorphism from the polynomial ring over $K$ in variables indexed by Lyndon words to $\mathcal{W}$ sending each variable to an element attached to its word (not expanded) is bijective. If the algebra structure is the shuffle product and the element is the word itself, this is Radford's theorem.

**(21)** *Entire functions without zeros on $\mathrm{Re}\,s\ge1$.* For every number field $K$ and finite set $S$ of primes: (i) for every unitary Hecke character $\chi$ not of the form $x\mapsto|x|^{it}$, $t\in\mathbb{R}$, there is an entire function without zeros on $\mathrm{Re}\,s\ge1$ that equals, for $\mathrm{Re}\,s>1$, a product $P^S_\chi(s)$ over the primes outside $S$ attached to $\chi$ (not expanded); (ii) there is an entire function without zeros on $\mathrm{Re}\,s\ge1$ equal for $\mathrm{Re}\,s>1$ to $(s-1)$ times the analogous product for constant data (not expanded). If $P^S_\chi$ is the partial Euler product, these are Hecke's theorem and the nonvanishing of Hecke $L$-functions and of the Dedekind zeta function on $\mathrm{Re}\,s=1$.

**(22)** *The logarithmic derivative of $L(s,\chi)$ at $s=1$.* There is a constant $C$ such that for every $q>1$ and every primitive Dirichlet character $\chi$ modulo $q$, $$\Bigl|\frac{L'(1,\chi)}{L(1,\chi)}-\sum_{\rho}\frac{m_\rho}{1-\rho}\Bigr|\le C\log(3q),$$ the sum being over the zeros $\rho$ of $L(s,\chi)$ with $0<\mathrm{Re}\,\rho<1$ and $|\mathrm{Im}\,\rho|\le1$, with natural numbers $m_\rho$ attached to them (not expanded). If $m_\rho$ is the multiplicity of the zero, this is a standard consequence of the Hadamard product for $L(s,\chi)$.

**(23)** *Five properties of an irreducible unitary representation of $U(\varepsilon)$.* For every irreducible unitary representation $\sigma$ of $U(\varepsilon)$ on a nonzero complete Hilbert space: a subspace attached to each subspace satisfying a condition is finite-dimensional; for $K$-finite $v$ the map $g\mapsto\sigma(g)v$ satisfies a condition on $U(\varepsilon)$; a subspace $H_K$ attached to $\sigma$ is dense; $H_K$ is stable under operators $d\sigma(X)$ for all $X$ in a set of matrices; and the only subspaces of $H_K$ stable under these operators are $0$ and $H_K$. The conditions, $H_K$, the operators and the set were not expanded. If $H_K$ is the space of $K$-finite vectors, $d\sigma$ the derived action of the Lie algebra, the first clause admissibility and the second analyticity, these are Harish-Chandra's theorems.

**(24)** *A $K$-type and uniqueness for discrete series representations.* For every Harish-Chandra parameter $\lambda$: in every discrete series representation of $U(\varepsilon)$ with parameter $\lambda$ a specific subspace built from $\lambda$ (not expanded) is a $K$-type with label $\lambda+\rho_n-\rho_c$; and any two discrete series representations of $U(\varepsilon)$ with parameter $\lambda$ are unitarily equivalent.

**(25)** *Structure of discrete series representations for parameters in one chamber.* Let $\lambda$ be a Harish-Chandra parameter with $\lambda_b<\lambda_a$ whenever $\varepsilon_a=+1$ and $\varepsilon_b=-1$, and $\sigma$ a discrete series representation of $U(\varepsilon)$ with parameter $\lambda$. Then: the label of every $K$-type is $\lambda+\rho_n-\rho_c$ plus a nonnegative integer combination of the vectors $e_a-e_b$ with $\varepsilon_a=+1$, $\varepsilon_b=-1$; every $K$-finite vector satisfies a condition; operators attached to the elements of a set of matrices annihilate the subspace of item (24); and a vector is $K$-finite if and only if it lies in a subspace built from that subspace. The condition, the operators, the set and the built subspace were not expanded. If they are smoothness, the action of $\mathfrak{p}^-$, and the span of the images under the enveloping algebra of $\mathfrak{p}^+$, this says that holomorphic discrete series are lowest weight modules.

**(26)** *Closures of certain subspaces; equivalence from an algebraic isomorphism.* (i) Let $\sigma$ be a unitary representation of $U(\varepsilon)$ on a complete Hilbert space and $V$ a subspace satisfying two conditions. Then the closure of $V$ is stable under $U(\varepsilon)$ and every $K$-finite vector of the closure lies in $V$. (ii) Let $\sigma_1,\sigma_2$ be irreducible unitary representations of $U(\varepsilon)$ on nonzero complete Hilbert spaces, with subspaces $H_{1},H_{2}$ attached to them, and $e:H_1\to H_2$ a linear isomorphism satisfying a condition. Then there are a unitary equivalence $U$ of $\sigma_1$ with $\sigma_2$ and $c>0$ with $U=c\,e$ on $H_1$. The conditions and the subspaces were not expanded. If the $H_i$ are the spaces of $K$-finite vectors and the conditions express stability, admissibility and compatibility with $K_\varepsilon$ and the Lie algebra, these are Harish-Chandra's theorems on admissible $(\mathfrak{g},K)$-modules and on infinitesimal equivalence.

**(27)** *Boundedness of cuspidal automorphic functions with their derivatives.* Let $D$ be a datum, $\theta$ an infinity family, $V$ a Hermitian space, and $\varphi$ an automorphic function on $G_V(\mathbb{A})$. For $\tau\in T$ and $X$ in a set $\mathfrak{l}_\tau$ of complex matrices, the Lean has a family $t\mapsto e_\tau(tX)$ of elements of $G_V(\mathbb{A})$ and an operator $R_{\tau,X}$ on functions (none expanded). Suppose $t\mapsto\varphi(g\,e_{\tau_1}(t_1X_1)\cdots e_{\tau_j}(t_jX_j))$ is smooth on $\mathbb{R}^j$ for all $g$ and all choices, and that for some $N$, every finite composite $R\varphi$ of the operators and every $x$ there is $C$ with $|R\varphi(xg)|\le C\,\|g\|^N$ for $g$ in a subset $G_\infty$, $\|\cdot\|$ being a function on it (subset and function not expanded). If $\varphi$ is cuspidal, every $R\varphi$ is bounded on $G_V(\mathbb{A})$. If $e_\tau$ is the exponential at the place $\tau$ and $R_{\tau,X}$ right differentiation, this says that cusp forms of moderate growth are bounded with all their derivatives.

**(29)** *Entire continuation, and a nonzero value at $s=1$.* For every number field $K$ and unitary Hecke character $\chi$, with $P_\chi(s)$ an infinite product of factors attached to $\chi$ and $s$ (not expanded): if $\chi$ is not of the form $x\mapsto|x|^{it}$, there is an entire function equal to $P_\chi(s)$ for $\mathrm{Re}\,s>1$; and if $\chi\ne1$, there is a function analytic at $s=1$, equal to $P_\chi(s)$ for $\mathrm{Re}\,s>1$, with nonzero value at $1$. If $P_\chi$ is the Euler product of $\chi$, these are the entire continuation of Hecke $L$-functions and their nonvanishing at $s=1$.

**(30)** *A double cover of the symplectic group with a unitary representation.* For every finite nonempty index set of size $n$, with $\mathcal{L}$ and $\mathcal{S}$ two function spaces attached to it and a map from $\mathcal{S}$ to $\mathcal{L}$, there are a topological group $G$, a surjective covering homomorphism $\mathrm{pr}:G\to\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(\mathbb{R})$ with kernel $\{1,\epsilon\}$, $\epsilon\ne1$, a homomorphism $\omega$ from $G$ to the linear isometric automorphisms of $\mathcal{L}$ and a homomorphism from $G$ to the continuous linear operators on $\mathcal{S}$ inducing $\omega$ on the image of $\mathcal{S}$, such that: $\omega(\epsilon)=-1$; $g\mapsto\omega(g)f$ is continuous for every $f$; $\omega(g)\rho(w)=\rho(\mathrm{pr}(g)w)\,\omega(g)$ for operators $\rho(w)$, $w\in\mathbb{R}^{2n}$; $t\mapsto\omega(\gamma(t))f$ is smooth for $f$ in the image of $\mathcal{S}$ and every continuous $\gamma:\mathbb{R}^j\to G$ with $\mathrm{pr}\circ\gamma$ smooth; and every $X$ in the symplectic Lie algebra has a continuous one-parameter subgroup $\gamma$ of $G$ with $\mathrm{pr}(\gamma(t))=\exp(tX)$ and $\frac{d}{dt}\omega(\gamma(t))f|_{t=0}=q_X f$ for $f\in\mathcal{S}$, $q_X$ being an operator attached to $X$. The spaces, $\rho$ and $q_X$ were not expanded. If $\mathcal{L}=L^2(\mathbb{R}^n)$, $\mathcal{S}$ is the Schwartz space and $\rho$ the Heisenberg operators, this is the existence of the metaplectic group with its Weil representation.

**(31)** *A uniform decay estimate.* Let $D$ be a datum, $\theta$ an infinity family, $V$ a Hermitian space, $U$ an open subgroup of $G_V(\mathbb{A})$ and $a,N\in\mathbb{N}$. In the notation of item (27), there are a finite set $\mathcal{L}$ of finite composites of the operators $R_{\tau,X}$ and a constant $C$ such that every cuspidal automorphic function $\varphi$ that is right invariant under $U\cap G_V^{f}$, satisfies the smoothness hypothesis of item (27), and all of whose composites $R\varphi$ satisfy a growth condition with exponent $a$ (not expanded), has the following property: if $|R\varphi(g)|\le c_R\,h(g)^a$ for all $R\in\mathcal{L}$ and all $g$, then $|\varphi(g)|\,h'(g)^N\le C\sum_{R\in\mathcal{L}}c_R$ for all $g$. Here $h$ and $h'$ are two functions on $G_V(\mathbb{A})$ that were not expanded.

**(32)** *A decomposition statement for a pair of groups.* For all $n,n'\ge1$, a condition holds both for $(\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathbb{C}),\mathrm{GL}_{n'}(\mathbb{C}))$ and for $(U(n),U(n'))$ with their inclusions: a representation attached to the pair is isomorphic to the direct sum, over an index set attached to $\min(n,n')$, of representations attached to the indices, and a condition relative to that index set holds for each of the two groups. None of these was expanded. If the representation is the space of polynomial functions on $n\times n'$ matrices, the index set that of partitions with at most $\min(n,n')$ parts, the summands the tensor products of the two irreducible representations with that highest weight, and the condition irreducibility and pairwise inequivalence, this is $(\mathrm{GL}_n,\mathrm{GL}_{n'})$ duality.

**(33)** *A representation of a local unitary group on functions on matrices.* Let $F$ be a nonarchimedean local field of characteristic $0$, $E$ a field that is an $F$-algebra with the module topology, $c$ an $F$-algebra endomorphism of $E$, and $\delta\in E^\times$ with $c(\delta)=-\delta$ such that $(1,\delta)$ is an $F$-basis of $E$. Let $Q$ be a matrix of size $m$ over $E$ with $c(Q)^{t}=Q$ and invertible determinant, $\psi$ a nontrivial continuous unitary character of $F$, and $\chi:E^\times\to S^1$ a continuous character with $\chi(a)=1$ for $a\in F^\times$ a norm from $E$ and $\chi(a)=(-1)^m$ for the other $a\in F^\times$. Let $U_{n,n}$ be the group of $g\in\mathrm{GL}_{2n}(E)$ with $g^{t}J\,c(g)=J$ for $J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}$. Then there is a smooth representation $\omega$ of $U_{n,n}$ on the locally constant compactly supported functions on $M_{m\times n}(E)$ which preserves the $L^2$ norm for every Haar measure, satisfies $\rho(w)\,\omega(g)=\omega(g)\,\rho(wg)$ for operators $\rho(w)$, $w\in M_{m\times2n}(E)$, built from $\psi$ and $Q$ (not expanded), and satisfies $$\bigl(\omega(m(a))\phi\bigr)(x)=\chi(\det a)\,|N_{E/F}\det a|^{m/2}\,\phi(xa)$$ for $a\in\mathrm{GL}_n(E)$, where $m(a)=\mathrm{diag}(a,\,c(a^{-1})^{t})$ and $|\cdot|$ is the normalised absolute value of $F$. If the $\rho(w)$ are the Heisenberg operators, this is Kudla's splitting of the Weil representation for the group $U_{n,n}$.

**(34)** *A double cover of the symplectic group with a representation and a Gaussian vector.* For every finite index set of size $n$ there are a topological group $G$, a surjective covering homomorphism $\mathrm{pr}:G\to\mathrm{Sp}_{2n}(\mathbb{R})$ with kernel of order $2$, a norm-preserving strongly continuous representation $\Omega$ of $G$ on $L^2(\mathbb{R}^n)$, a jointly continuous representation $\omega$ of $G$ on the Schwartz space inducing $\Omega$, and a Schwartz function $\phi_0$, such that: $\omega(g)\rho(w)=\rho(\mathrm{pr}(g)\cdot w)\,\omega(g)$ for operators $\rho(w)$ and an action of symplectic matrices on the $w$ (neither expanded); the nontrivial element of the kernel acts by $-1$; $\phi_0(x)=e^{-\pi x\cdot x}$; for all $g$, $\bigl(\int\omega(g)\phi_0\,\overline{\phi_0}\bigr)^2\det h(\mathrm{pr}\,g)=\bigl(\int|\phi_0|^2\bigr)^2$, where $h$ is a complex $n\times n$ matrix built from a symplectic matrix (not expanded); and if $\mathrm{pr}(g)$ is an orthogonal matrix then $\omega(g)\phi_0=c\,\phi_0$ with $c^2\det h(\mathrm{pr}\,g)=1$. Here $L^2$ and the Schwartz space are the standard ones. If the $\rho(w)$ are the Heisenberg operators, this is the metaplectic representation with the transformation law of the Gaussian.

**(37)** *The numbers $j(\mathfrak{a})$ form one orbit.* Let $K\subset\mathbb{C}$ be an imaginary quadratic field and $\mathfrak{a}$ a fractional ideal. Then $j(\mathfrak{a})$ is algebraic over $K$, and its orbit under the group of $K$-algebra automorphisms of $\mathbb{C}$ is the set of all $j(\mathfrak{b})$, $\mathfrak{b}$ a fractional ideal of $K$.

**(38)** *Hasse principle for Hermitian forms.* Let $E/F$ be a quadratic extension of number fields with nontrivial automorphism $\sigma$ and $G,G'$ nondegenerate $\sigma$-Hermitian matrices of size $r$ over $E$. If for every finite and every infinite place $v$ of $F$ there is a matrix $P$ over $F_v\otimes_FE$ with invertible determinant and $G'=P^{t}G\,\sigma(P)$, then there is such a $P$ over $E$. This is Landherr's theorem. The statement is entirely in terms of standard objects.

**(39)** *Existence and uniqueness of a representation with given central character.* Let $F$ be a nonarchimedean local field with $2\ne0$, $W$ a finite-dimensional $F$-vector space with a nondegenerate alternating form, and $\psi$ a nontrivial continuous additive character of $F$. Let $\mathcal{H}(W)$ be the set of pairs of a vector and a scalar, with a group structure that was not expanded.
