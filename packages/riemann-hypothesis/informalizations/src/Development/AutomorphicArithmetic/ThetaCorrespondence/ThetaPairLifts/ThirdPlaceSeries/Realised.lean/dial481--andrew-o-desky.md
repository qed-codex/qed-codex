## Transfer conditions and numerical bounds for the stage-zero construction

[Definition / hypothesis THETA.D091](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/GlobalTheory.Fc.S0.OpenParameterStatements/)

This entry uses the notions of datum, family, class, refinement and sufficiently constrained family from ([Data, families of data, classes of families, and properties of all sufficiently constrained families of a class](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.MainNotions.Closed/)), and point, attached record, selection and transfer from ([The norm bound at the primes 2 and 3 for the families produced by the construction with a given parameter](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/GlobalTheory.Fc.RL.LinkedAt/)). The latter entry defines transfer through a property of points and a link. Here the construction parameters may be arbitrary: they determine a built-point property, a good-point property and a link, just as in that entry. For a property $H$ of a class and a selection, say **$H$ transfers** if there is a property $R$ of points such that, eventually for large bases satisfying the base condition, every built point has $R$, and $H$ holds for the families produced through the good points satisfying $R$ and the link. The base condition and the internal construction are not expanded.

**Twelve open conditions.** Each of the following properties must transfer. In the first seven and the ninth, it is required at every member of every family of the class, for its selected record. In the eighth, tenth and eleventh it is required eventually at members of every sufficiently constrained family of the second refinement. In the twelfth it is required there for every sufficiently large real constant, above a nonnegative threshold depending on the family. The properties are:

1. Two conditions on the selected record, one on its realisation and one on separation of types (not expanded).
2. A condition on subspaces attached to the selected record (not expanded).
3. A condition relating lattices and pairings in the datum and the selected record (not expanded).
4. A condition comparing pairings at decomposition points; its form is given in item (8) of ([The record of conditions on a class of families that the final argument takes as input](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/GlobalTheory.Fc.HypsAlt/)).
5. Existence of a record relating the selected realisation to a choice of indices (the record is not expanded).
6. A compatibility condition on the selected record (not expanded).
7. Existence, at each selected record, of three objects with three compatibility conditions (the objects and conditions are not expanded).
8. A condition on the datum (not expanded).
9. Existence of a comparison object for the selected record satisfying four conditions, the fourth itself a conjunction of three conditions; these conditions are not expanded.
10. A condition on multiplicities in the selected realisation (not expanded).
11. A condition on numbers attached to the selected record (not expanded).
12. The definite-side condition of item (25) of ([The record of conditions on a class of families that the final argument takes as input](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/GlobalTheory.Fc.HypsAlt/)), with the two constants just quantified.

**Two numerical statements.** For all sufficiently constrained families satisfying an additional list of conditions (not expanded), consider points at members with $x>x_1$ that satisfy a point condition (not expanded). The first statement assumes the divisibility bound of ([A class of families satisfying the input conditions of the final argument exists, given hypothesis list A, the rationality test, the norm bound at 2 and 3, a maximal slope bound and a covolume bound](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/GlobalTheory.Fc.S0.StageU.hypotheses_nonempty_of_unramified_exponents_alt/)). It asserts the existence of $C_0\ge0$ such that, eventually at these members, for every such point satisfying three further conditions (not expanded) and every $C\ge C_0$, the following holds. For its finite set $T$ of places, indices $j$, tuples $\mu_w$, and numerical data $I_{j,t,w}, r_w, F_{w,j}, e_{w,j}, q_w, J_{t,w,j}, L_w, D, E$ (not expanded), $$\sum_j(I_{j,\mu_w,w}+I_{j,\mu_w,\bar w})\le D\left(\sum_j(2r_wF_{w,j}+r_we_{w,j})+\frac{2r_w}{q_w^2}\right),\quad e_{w,j}\ge0\quad(w\in T),$$ $$\sum_{w\in T}L_w\bigl(r_we_{w,j}+r_wJ_{\mu_w,w,j}\bigr)\le C E\quad\text{for every }j.$$ The second statement is the lower bound for the scalars of ([Admissible points over the members of a family, the row lattice of a point and its maximal slope](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/GlobalTheory.BigTop.FamHyp/)): eventually at the admissible points of the family, for every real $C$, $$l_6(j)\le\log\lambda_{j,\mu_0,\pi,v}\quad\text{for all stages }j,\text{ full labels }\pi\text{ and infinite places }v.$$ That page defines the class of families, the admissible points, the numbers $l_6(j)$ formed with $C$, and the scalars $\lambda_{j,\mu_0,\pi,v}$. Both statements quantify over all sufficiently constrained families of the additional class, not merely over families arising from the construction. The first numerical statement has the inequalities displayed above; its body does not assert surjectivity.

**The 26 transferred conditions.** The conclusion of the theorem using this entry consists of the following assertions. Each uses the transfer quantifiers above, except for the placement of counting constants specified below. The first eleven concern properties of the class independent of selection: the class-number formula of item (10), the surjectivity of item (23), the mass and class assertions of item (22), and the seven eventual conditions of items (26)–(32), followed by the compatibility condition of item (37), all from ([The record of conditions on a class of families that the final argument takes as input](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/GlobalTheory.Fc.HypsAlt/)). These descriptions refer to the properties themselves; their quantifiers over families are those given in the cited items, except that the mass and class assertions here hold at every member of every family in the class.

The remaining fifteen are properties of the class with its selection. They comprise: a bound on the realisation and the condition of item (4); the five conditions (1)–(5) of the twelve open conditions above; two eventual conditions on the selection (not expanded); the serving condition of item (33); the surjectivity condition of item (36); the condition of item (14); a counting condition; the nine bounds and definite-side condition of item (25); and an arbitrary property $N$ of the second refinement of the class. All numbered items in this paragraph refer to ([The record of conditions on a class of families that the final argument takes as input](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/GlobalTheory.Fc.HypsAlt/)). For the counting condition, after the property $R$ of points is chosen, there are a function assigning counting constants to each finite list of reals and a number $c_I>0$, with $C_0\ge0$ and $2(C_{YL}+1)\le A_0$ for every family. The conjunction of two counting properties then holds for the families produced through the good points satisfying $R$: a count at every member using those constants (not expanded), and the existence of the objects and count of item (35), for all sufficiently constrained families of the second refinement. The other terms in these conditions are not expanded beyond the cited entry.

## Admissible points over the members of a family, the row lattice of a point and its maximal slope

[Definition / hypothesis ARAK.D271](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/GlobalTheory.BigTop.FamHyp/)

Several theorems of the proof assert a property of the points over the members of a family, for all members beyond a bound. This page defines the class of families $\mathcal C$, points and admissible points, the phrase "eventually at the admissible points", the row lattice of a point and its maximal slope, the divisibility bound for a family, and the lower bound for the scalars.

**Families.** Datum, size data, family with parameter $\varepsilon$, member, starting scale $x_1$, class of families, and "holds for all sufficiently constrained families of a class" are as in ([Data, families of data, classes of families, and properties of all sufficiently constrained families of a class](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.MainNotions.Closed/)). At a member $(x,i)$ we write $k,m,n,f$ for the numbers of its datum, $K$ for its coefficient field (written $\Lambda$ on that page), $s_K$ for the number of infinite places of $K$, and $b=\exp(x^{\varepsilon})$. Throughout, $\mathcal C$ is the class of the families that satisfy the fourteen standard conditions (N1)–(N4) and (a)–(j) of that page. These are the fourteen conditions on a family in ([Covolume of integral cusp forms](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/GlobalTheory.CovTop.CovolumeBoundOfX/)).

**Points.** Let $(x,i)$ be a member of a family with $x>x_1$. A *point over $(x,i)$* consists of the choices of ([Compatible points for arithmetic covolume bounds](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/GlobalTheory.BigTop.PtAt/)), subject to the conditions of that page, for the arithmetic member $(x,i)$ with its local and theta data and for the height constant $C_{\rm size}$ of the family. A point contains a row-side family in the sense of ([Row-side families of Hermitian spaces](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.L2.Rows.RowSideFamily/)), with its tuple set $\mathcal T$ and its base tuple, written $\mu_0$ here; analytic realisation data; and the coordinate data of the base level and of the sharp level that are computed from the analytic realisation.

**Notation at a point.** For the coordinate data we use the notation of ([Row-side families of Hermitian spaces](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.L2.Rows.RowSideFamily/)) with the following abbreviations. At the base level $b=0$: $d=d_0$ is the common dimension; $X_v=X_{E,0,v}$ is the frame matrix at an infinite place $v$ of $K$; $\mu(w)\in\mathcal T$ is the tuple assigned to a finite place $w$ of $K$; for $t\in\mathcal T$, $P_t=P_{0,t}$ is the projection, $M_t=M_{0,t}$ the integral module and $\iota_t=\iota_{0,t}$ the matrix with values in $K^{d}$; and $\kappa_v=\kappa_{0,v}$. At the sharp level $b=1$: $\lambda_{j,t,\pi,v}=\lambda_{1,j,t,\pi,v}$ for a stage $j$, a tuple $t$, a full label $\pi$ and an infinite place $v$. For real numbers $C$ and $C_H$, the *numerical data of the point with constants $(C,C_H)$* are those of that page; among them are $$h=x^{C_H}\,b,\qquad l_6(j)=l_{6,m}(j)-C\,l_{6,u}.$$

**The row lattice.** For an $\mathcal O_K$-submodule $L$ of $K^{d}$ and a finite place $w$ of $K$ with prime ideal $\mathfrak p_w$, put $$L_{(w)}=\{\,y\in K^{d}\ :\ s\,y\in L\ \text{for some } s\in\mathcal O_K\smallsetminus\mathfrak p_w\,\}.$$ The *row lattice* of a point is the $\mathcal O_K$-submodule $$R_0=\bigcap_{w}\bigl(\iota_{\mu(w)}(P_{\mu(w)}M_{\mu(w)})\bigr)_{(w)}\ \subseteq\ K^{d},$$ where $w$ runs over the finite places of $K$. It is the module $R_0$ of ([Base target slope from determinant and local index bounds](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.L2.Indef.IndefSide.boundsMu/)), formed from the coordinate data of the base level.

**Maximal slope.** For $y\in K^{d}$ and an infinite place $v$ of $K$, let $y_v\in\mathbb C^{d}$ be the image of $y$ under the embedding chosen for $v$, and let $\phi(y)=(X_v^{-1}y_v)_v$, an element of the real Euclidean space $\prod_v\mathbb C^{d}$ with its standard inner product. For a nonzero $\mathcal O_K$-submodule $N$ of $K^{d}$ put $$\sigma(N)=-\frac{\log\operatorname{covol}\phi(N)}{\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Z}\phi(N)},$$ where $\operatorname{covol}$ is the square root of the Gram determinant of a $\mathbb Z$-basis. The *maximal slope* of the row lattice is $$\mu_{\max}(R_0)=2\,\sup\{\sigma(N)\ :\ N\subseteq R_0\text{ a nonzero }\mathcal O_K\text{-submodule}\}-\log 2+\frac{\log|\operatorname{disc}K|}{[K:\mathbb Q]} .$$ When $K$ is totally complex and $N$ is a full lattice in a $K$-subspace, the number $2\sigma(N)-\log2+\log|\operatorname{disc}K|/[K:\mathbb Q]$ is the slope $\mu_X(N)$ defined in the conventions of ([Base target slope from determinant and local index bounds](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.L2.Indef.IndefSide.boundsMu/)).

**Admissible points.** A point is *admissible* if its analytic realisation data, with the further choices of the point, satisfy the following seven conditions. The objects named in them are those of the pages cited.

1. Hypothesis (existence of analytic theta maps) of ([Analytic theta maps and primitive row comparisons](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.IndefR.Links.RealDat.ExAt/)).
2. At every stage and at every embedding $K\to\mathbb C$ that is not good, some unit vector of the discrete-series model evaluates to the complex conjugate automorphic form.
3. At every stage there is an action of the finite group on the cusp forms over the field of the original moduli that preserves the integral forms and that, at every embedding $K\to\mathbb C$, corresponds to the complex action multiplied by the designated character.
4. At every stage and at every good embedding $K\to\mathbb C$, evaluation against the chosen extremal vector is injective.
5. At every stage there is a tuple of unit lowest-type vectors of the discrete-series model at the distinguished embedding, each of the prescribed extremal weight, that pairs with a coefficient vector of norm $1$ to the determinant polynomial of ([Analytic theta intertwiners and Weil actions](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.IndefR.Links.RealDat.IsChainW/)).
6. At every stage, Hypothesis (one-map rows) of ([Geometric theta class evaluations](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.IndefR.FWT.OMPins/)).
7. Hypothesis (primitive row comparisons) of ([Analytic theta maps and primitive row comparisons](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.IndefR.Links.RealDat.ExAt/)).

An embedding $\sigma\colon K\to\mathbb C$ is *good* if its restriction to the coefficient field $K_s$ of the original moduli is allowed in the sense of ([One-map and geometric comparison laws](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.IndefR.FWT.FWTie/)), that is, if it sends the designated square root in $K_s$ to the fixed complex square root. Conditions 2 to 5 are four of the conditions of ([Compatibility conditions for covolume points](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/GlobalTheory.BigTop.PtAt.CompatibilityConditions/)), which imposes the seven conditions together with further ones; the objects of these four conditions are those of that page.

**Eventually at the admissible points.** Let $\mathrm{Fm}$ be a family and $Q$ a property of points. We say that *$Q$ holds eventually at the admissible points of $\mathrm{Fm}$* if there is a real number $x_0$ such that $Q$ holds for every admissible point over every member $(x,i)$ of $\mathrm{Fm}$ with $x>x_1$ and $x\ge x_0$.

**The divisibility bound.** A family *satisfies the divisibility bound* if there are real numbers $C$ and $x_0$ such that at every member with $x\ge x_0$ and for every $j\in\{1,\dots,m\}$, $$\sum_{w}\frac{\log\mathrm N\mathfrak p_w}{[K:\mathbb Q]}\,e_w\,\eta_j(w)\ \le\ D_0\cdot C\,x^{\varepsilon}\bigl(f\,m\,b\,x^{\varepsilon}+N_c\bigr),\qquad N_c=\frac{|T_2|\,k\,b\,x^{2\varepsilon}}{n^{2}} .$$ Here $w$ runs over the finite places of $K$ in the finite set $\mathcal S$ of ([Local size and support data](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.BasicNotions.SizeData%E2%82%82/)) to which the datum assigns a tuple different from its base tuple; $e_w$ is the ramification index of $w$ over $\mathbb Q$; $\eta_j(w)$ is the non-negative rational number $\eta_{j,w}$ of ([Complex vector spaces and integral spans](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.BasicNotions.Rows.Data/)), and $D_0$ is the dimension of the space $H_0$ of that page; $T_2$ is the finite set of ([Family fields and Galois data](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.HC.Frame/)).

**The lower bound for the scalars.** This is the following statement. For all sufficiently constrained families $\mathrm{Fm}$ of $\mathcal C$, with the real number $C_H$ of that definition, the following holds eventually at the admissible points of $\mathrm{Fm}$: for every real number $C$, the numerical data of the point with constants $(C,C_H)$ satisfy $$l_6(j)\ \le\ \log\lambda_{j,\mu_0,\pi,v}$$ for every stage $j$, every full label $\pi$ and every infinite place $v$ of $K$.

## Compatibility conditions for covolume points

[Definition / hypothesis ARAK.D170](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/GlobalTheory.BigTop.PtAt.CompatibilityConditions/)

Hypothesis (compatibility conditions for covolume points). Use ([Compatible points for arithmetic covolume bounds](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/GlobalTheory.BigTop.PtAt/)) and ([Coefficient fields for covolume comparisons](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/GlobalTheory.FinalJoin2.FieldOK0/)) for the same arithmetic member and height constant. Impose ([Canonical integral modules for original moduli](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.L2.Pj.LawsM.pins/)), ([Integral evaluation of moduli sections](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.L2.Pj.LawsM.evalWD/)), ([Original moduli uniformizations and cusp norms](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.L2.Pj.LawsM/)), and ([Isotropic lattices and Fourier exponents](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.L2.Pj.FJ.LatZ/)) on its original moduli realization. Every natural stage, including stages outside $1,\ldots,m$, has positive polarized-module rank.

Require ([Compatibility of recursive coordinate realizations](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.IndefR.IndefiniteRealisation/)), using ([Linked arithmetic and theta realizations](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.L2.TiedDataSKAlt/)) and ([Spectral column and period realizations](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.L2.SG.HidaSetting.HidaSetting/)). Reconstruct the point from those choices, its selected columns and periods, its values of $\Delta,h_0$, and its stabilizer divisibilities. The reconstructed point equals the chosen point, including every chosen object and comparison.

The two chosen quadratic primes above $g_0,g_1$ in the original moduli realization equal the primes in ([Hecke characters in recursive realizations](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.L2.Host.CharFrame/)). The original level ideal is the product of the prime ideals in the analytic generator-place set. The conjugate of each fixed norm-$g_i$ error generator belongs to the extension of the corresponding chosen quadratic prime. Both fixed auxiliary Hecke characters take all values in $F_O=\mathbb Q(\jmath(O))$.

At every nonbase coefficient place $w$ put $p_D(w)=p'_\Delta$ when its residue characteristic is $p_\Delta$, and $p_D(w)=p_\Delta$ otherwise. For each of these two excluded rational primes choose a prime of the quadratic subfield above it, using the same choice for every $w$ with that $p_D(w)$. Its extension to $E$ is contained in every auxiliary prime of the selected tuple above $p_D(w)$.

Impose ([Analytic theta maps and primitive row comparisons](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.IndefR.Links.RealDat.ExAt/)), ([Integral frames and Hecke operator laws](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.L2.DIV.DefFam3.Ok3/)), ([Divisor rows and additive-character comparisons](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.L2.DIV.DivisionCompatibilityDataThreeSided/)), ([One-map and geometric comparison laws](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.IndefR.FWT.FWTie/)), ([Geometric theta class evaluations](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.IndefR.FWT.OMPins/)), and ([Scalar normalizations and series comparisons](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/GlobalTheory.SeriesTie.ExportTie/)). In particular, at each good coefficient embedding evaluation against the chosen extremal vector is injective. At each other coefficient embedding a unit vector evaluates to the complex conjugate automorphic form. At each stage a rational action of the finite group on cusp forms preserves the integral lattice and realizes its complex action multiplied by its designated character. The extremal vector, expressed as a tuple of lowest-type vectors, pairs with a unit coefficient vector to the explicit determinant polynomial in ([Analytic theta intertwiners and Weil actions](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.IndefR.Links.RealDat.IsChainW/)).

Any two different occurring base Hecke types, including their inert eigenvalues, remain distinguished at every lifted stage: their transported types differ either on an allowed split Hecke operator or on an allowed inert operator. The member's designated distinguished dimension is the dimension of the selected base-tuple rational row subspace in principal-level coordinates, as defined in ([Class sums and finite Hecke families](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.L2.DIV.CSD/)).

Every nonzero local defect is supported in the designated twisted-place set. More explicitly, if for some stage either base image index at $w$ or $\bar w$, either local determinant index of the selected base lattice at $w$ or $\bar w$, or either local error allocation at $w$ or $\bar w$ is nonzero, then $w$ belongs to that twisted set.

The chosen subgroup equals the subgroup used by the theta expansions. The inverse of its selected principal-level character has values represented by elements of the embedded base field $K_0$; the oriented coefficient embeddings of these elements are the specified theta-expansion characters for every twist index and side. Every theta-expansion input vector transforming under that subgroup by this character belongs to the selected expansion-input set. The field $K_0\subset K$ is the chosen intermediate coefficient field of ([Identifications for arithmetic family members](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.MainNotions.N.LocTie/)); it is separate from the field $F_O$ generated by the integral ring image.

For every nonbase coefficient place, all values of its local twisting Hecke character, with zero absolute-value twist, lie in the designated embedded base field $K_0$. That field contains a copy of the ray class field of the chosen quadratic field of modulus $g_0g_1p_\Delta p'_\Delta$, extending the given quadratic-field embedding. It contains every root of $z^{g_i(g_i-1)}=1$ for $i=0,1$.

For a tuple $t$, put $$M_t=g_0g_1\prod_{q\in\{NP:P\in Q_t\}}q,$$ using each distinct residue cardinality once. Under the fixed quadratic-field embedding into $K$, every prime ideal $\mathfrak P$ of $K$ not containing $M_t$, with contraction $\mathfrak p$, admits $a\in\mathbb N$ and $\alpha\in\mathcal O_{E_0}$ satisfying $$N\mathfrak P=(N\mathfrak p)^a,\qquad \mathfrak p^a=(\alpha),\qquad \alpha\equiv1\pmod{M_t}.$$

Impose the memberwise conditions in ([Integral twisting conditions for arithmetic families](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.MainNotions.TwIn/)) with the effective twist character replaced by every integer power $\Psi^a$ with $\gcd(|a|,\ell)=1$ at each nonbase coefficient place of residue characteristic $\ell$. One choice of all $N_0(i)$ and all finite expansion subsets works simultaneously for every coefficient place and every such power; the error allocations may depend on the power. The conjugate-place twist data and their orientation conditions are also imposed.

Every finite-type label has a selected tuple of generator-place polynomials, using the definition of selected in ([Finite types and idempotents for the family](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.BasicNotions.NTypes/)). Thus its polynomial tuple satisfies that definition at both generator primes and for both the selected tame characters and their inverses.

For each level prime $g$ choose a unital multiplicative map $\vartheta:\overline{\mathbb F}_g\to\mathbb C$ which is injective on nonzero elements, and elements $\zeta_e\in\overline{\mathbb F}_g$ for every natural $e$, with $\zeta_e$ of order $g^e-1$ for $e\ge1$, satisfying $\zeta_{eh}^{(g^{eh}-1)/(g^e-1)}=\zeta_e$ for $e,h\ge1$. A monic irreducible polynomial $Q$ with nonzero constant term labels the cuspidal characters in ([Cuspidal characters of finite general linear groups](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/GlobalTheory.greenRegularCharacters_holds/)) by the parameter $\theta_Q(\zeta_e)=\vartheta(\alpha)$, for a root $\alpha$ of $Q$. An irreducible character $\chi$ has label $F$ if it occurs in Harish–Chandra induction of a list of these cuspidal characters whose ranks sum to $r$ and whose polynomials multiply to $F$. The prescribed central idempotents are required to be $$e_F=\sum_{\substack{\chi\text{ irreducible of }G\\\chi\text{ has label }F}}\frac{\chi(1)}{|G|}\sum_{h\in G}\chi(h^{-1})[h],\qquad G=\mathrm{GL}_r(\mathbb F_g),$$ using the prescribed $\vartheta$. Here $[h]$ is the basis element of $\mathbb C[G]$.

For every generator place over $g$ and each stage $j+1$, let $c_{v,j}\in\mathbb F_g^\times$ be its designated tame parameter. The degree-one character determined by $c_{v,j}$ through the chosen $\zeta_1,\vartheta$ is either $\omega_g^{2(j+1)}$ or its inverse, where $\omega_g$ is the chosen injective generator character in ([Hecke characters in recursive realizations](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.L2.Host.CharFrame/)). The finite-field embedding $\vartheta$ equals the member’s prescribed one at each generator prime.
