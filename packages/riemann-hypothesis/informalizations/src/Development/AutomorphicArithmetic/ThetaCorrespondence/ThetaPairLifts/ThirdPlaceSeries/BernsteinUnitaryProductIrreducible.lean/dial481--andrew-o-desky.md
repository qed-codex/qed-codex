## Hypothesis list A: sixty assumed statements on representations of p-adic and real groups, automorphic forms, Galois representations and class field theory

[Definition / hypothesis AGR.D100](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/GlobalTheory.Fc.S0.UnitaryGroupThirdPlaceHypotheses/)

Assume all sixty statements below.

**Setting and notation.**

*Local fields.* Throughout, $k$ is a nonarchimedean local field, of any characteristic unless characteristic $0$ is stated; $\mathcal{O}_k$ is its ring of integers and $q_k$ the cardinality of its residue field. Representations of $\mathrm{GL}_n(k)$ are on complex vector spaces without topology. Such a representation is *smooth* if every vector has an open stabiliser, *admissible* if it is smooth and every compact open subgroup has a finite-dimensional space of fixed vectors, *unitarisable* if it has an invariant positive definite Hermitian form, and *irreducible* if its only invariant subspaces are $0$ and the whole space. $K_0=\mathrm{GL}_n(\mathcal{O}_k)$. For a decomposition $n=n_1+\dots+n_s$ let $M=\prod_i\mathrm{GL}_{n_i}(k)$; the Lean attaches to the decomposition a subgroup $P$ of $\mathrm{GL}_n(k)$ with a projection onto $M$ (not expanded), and $N$ is the kernel of that projection. For a representation $\rho$ of $M$, $i_P(\rho)$ is the representation of $\mathrm{GL}_n(k)$ induced from $P$ by an induction functor (not expanded) from the inflation of $\rho$ twisted by a fixed character of $M$ (not expanded). For a representation $\pi$ of $\mathrm{GL}_n(k)$, $\pi_N$ is a quotient of the space of $\pi$ attached to $N$ (not expanded), and $r_N(\pi)$ is the representation of $M$ on $\pi_N$ (not expanded) twisted by the inverse of the same character. If $P$ is the block upper triangular subgroup, the induction is smooth induction, the character is $\delta_P^{1/2}$ and $\pi_N$ is the space of $N$-coinvariants, these are normalised parabolic induction and the normalised Jacquet module. We write $\pi_1\times\pi_2=i_P(\pi_1\otimes\pi_2)$ for a decomposition into two blocks; the product $\pi_1\times\dots\times\pi_s$ of more factors is a separate construction of the Lean that was not expanded. The Lean has a predicate on representations of $\mathrm{GL}_d(k)$ that was not expanded; we write *$\mathcal{C}$-representation* for a representation satisfying it. If it is cuspidality, a $\mathcal{C}$-representation is a cuspidal one. A *segment* $\Delta$ consists of an isomorphism class of irreducible smooth representations of some $\mathrm{GL}_d(k)$, $d\ge1$, that contains a $\mathcal{C}$-representation, and a length $\ell$; the remaining components of the record were not read. $K_1\subset K_0$ is the kernel of reduction to the residue field. An irreducible smooth representation $\pi$ of $\mathrm{GL}_n(k)$ has *$\mathcal{C}$-support* $(\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_s)$ if each $\sigma_a$ is an irreducible $\mathcal{C}$-representation of some $\mathrm{GL}_{d_a}(k)$ with $d_a\ge1$ and $\pi$ stands in a relation (not expanded) to $\sigma_1\times\dots\times\sigma_s$ taken in some order; if the relation is that of being a subquotient, this is the cuspidal support.

*Real unitary groups.* For $\varepsilon\in\{\pm1\}^n$ put $J_\varepsilon=\operatorname{diag}(\varepsilon)$ and $U(\varepsilon)=\{g\in\operatorname{GL}_n(\mathbb C):g^*J_\varepsilon g=J_\varepsilon\}$. Let $K_\varepsilon$ be the unitary matrices commuting with $J_\varepsilon$. A unitary representation $\sigma$ on a complex inner product space is a strongly continuous representation by norm-preserving bounded complex-linear operators. It is irreducible if its only closed invariant complex subspaces are zero and the whole space. It is square-integrable if $g\mapsto\langle w,\sigma(g)v\rangle$ belongs to $L^2$ of Haar measure for every $v,w$. A vector is $K$-finite if it lies in a finite-dimensional $K_\varepsilon$-invariant complex subspace. For a real array $\mu$, its torus-weight equation is $$\sigma(\operatorname{diag}(e^{\mathrm i\theta_a}))v=e^{\mathrm i\sum_a\mu_a\theta_a}v\qquad(\theta\in\mathbb R^n).$$ A $K$-type of highest weight $\Lambda$ is a finite-dimensional irreducible $K_\varepsilon$-invariant complex subspace containing a nonzero vector with this weight, such that every weight $\mu$ of a nonzero vector in it satisfies $$\Lambda-\mu=\sum_{a<b,\ \varepsilon_a=\varepsilon_b}c_{ab}(e_a-e_b),\qquad c_{ab}\in\mathbb Z_{\ge0},$$ where $e_a$ are the standard basis vectors of $\mathbb R^n$. A Harish–Chandra parameter is a strictly decreasing $\lambda\in\mathbb R^n$ with $\lambda_a-(n-1)/2\in\mathbb Z$ for every $a$. Put $$\rho_c(a)=\tfrac12\bigl(\#\{b>a:\varepsilon_b=\varepsilon_a\}-\#\{b<a:\varepsilon_b=\varepsilon_a\}\bigr),$$ and define $\rho_n(a)$ by replacing equality of signs by inequality. For a real array $\lambda$, a discrete series representation with parameter $\lambda$ is an irreducible square-integrable unitary representation on a nonzero complex Hilbert space, containing a $K$-type of highest weight $\kappa=\lambda+\rho_n-\rho_c$, with $$\|\kappa+2\rho_c\|^2\le\|\mu+2\rho_c\|^2$$ for every occurring $K$-type highest weight $\mu$. The norms in this formula are Euclidean.

*Number fields.* For a number field $E$, $\mathbb{A}_E$ is its adele ring. A *Hecke character* of $E$ is a continuous homomorphism $\mathbb{A}_E^\times\to\mathbb{C}^\times$ trivial on $E^\times$; it is *unitary* if its values have absolute value $1$. For a CM field $E$, $E^+$ is its maximal totally real subfield and $c$ its complex conjugation. A *CM extension* $E/F$ is a totally complex quadratic extension of a totally real number field, with an $F$-endomorphism $c$ of $E$ inducing complex conjugation under every complex embedding.

*The datum $D$ and its unitary groups.* A datum $D$ consists of the choices satisfying ([Identifications for arithmetic family members](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.MainNotions.N.LocTie/)) and its cited hypotheses. Its rank and field data are those of ([Family fields and Galois data](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.HC.Frame/)). Write $E$ for its CM field, $F$ for its maximal real subfield, $c$ for conjugation on $E$, and $T$ for its finite set of embedding labels, identified with the selected CM type. Write $\mathbb A=\mathbb A_F$, $\mathbb A_E=\mathbb A\otimes_FE$, and $F_\infty$ for the infinite adele ring of $F$. A Hermitian space means a Hermitian space with recorded lattice data as defined in ([Hermitian spaces in the theta constructions](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.BasicNotions.CMData/)); $n_V$ is its $E$-dimension, and its Witt index is the largest dimension of an $E$-subspace on which its form vanishes. $G_V(\mathbb{A})$ is a group of invertible matrices over $\mathbb{A}_E$ defined from $c$ and the Gram matrix of $V$ (one level opened; if the definition below it is the usual one, this is the adelic unitary group of $V$). $\Gamma_V$ is a subgroup of $G_V(\mathbb{A})$ and $G_V^{f}$ the kernel of a homomorphism out of $G_V(\mathbb{A})$; neither was expanded. If $\Gamma_V$ is the group of rational points and the homomorphism is the projection to the infinite places, then $[G_V]=\Gamma_V\backslash G_V(\mathbb{A})$ is the usual automorphic quotient and $G_V^f$ the finite adelic points. An *automorphic function* on $G_V(\mathbb{A})$ is a continuous function, left invariant under $\Gamma_V$ and right invariant under $U\cap G_V^{f}$ for some open subgroup $U$. It is *cuspidal* if for every matrix $C$ over $E$ satisfying a condition relative to $V$, with attached groups $N_C\supset\Gamma_C$, every Haar measure on $N_C$ and every fundamental domain $\mathcal{F}$ of $\Gamma_C$ in $N_C$, one has $\int_{\mathcal{F}}\varphi(ng)\,dn=0$ for all $g$; the condition and the groups were not expanded. If the $N_C$ are the adelic unipotent radicals of the parabolic subgroups of $G_V$, this is the usual cuspidality. An infinity family is a compatible family of archimedean ring homomorphisms in ([Weil actions and prescribed theta test vectors](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.IndefR.WeilFull.IsWeilFull/)), indexed by $T$. $V^\square$ denotes a second Hermitian space built from $V$ (not expanded). Write $W_{S,S'}$ for the matrix space of the pair $(S,S')$ in ([Weil actions and prescribed theta test vectors](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.IndefR.WeilFull.IsWeilFull/)). An element $a\in\mathrm{GL}_{n_V}(\mathbb{A}_E)$ is *adapted to $V$* if it satisfies a condition that was not expanded; it determines a subgroup $\hat K_a$ of $G_{V^\square}(\mathbb{A})$ (not expanded). The *Iwasawa decomposition holds for $\hat K_a$* if every element of $G_{V^\square}(\mathbb{A})$ is a product $pk$ with $k\in\hat K_a$ and $p$ in a fixed subgroup $P_V$ (not expanded). For a character $\chi$ and natural number $m$, use the prescribed restriction in degree $m$ in ([Weil actions and prescribed theta test vectors](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.IndefR.WeilFull.IsWeilFull/)). Theta functions and Weil representations for $(\psi,S,S',\chi_S,\chi_{S'})$ have the definitions given there.

*Automorphic representations of general linear groups.* A *$\mathcal{K}$-representation* of $\mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{A}_E)$ is a nonzero space of smooth functions on $\mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{A}_E)$ whose elements satisfy two conditions, which is stable under a family of operators, and which has no proper nonzero stable subspace; the two conditions and the operators were not expanded. If the conditions are those defining automorphic forms and cuspidality and the operators are those of the adelic group and its Lie algebra, a $\mathcal{K}$-representation is an irreducible cuspidal automorphic representation, realised in a space of cusp forms. To a $\mathcal{K}$-representation $\pi$ and an embedding $\tau:E\to\mathbb{C}$ the Lean attaches a multiset $c_\tau(\pi)$ of complex numbers: one satisfying with $(\pi,\tau)$ a condition that was not expanded if such a multiset exists, and the multiset of $a$ zeros otherwise. If the condition says that the multiset is the infinitesimal character of $\pi$ at $\tau$, then $c_\tau(\pi)$ is that infinitesimal character. $\pi$ is *of type $\mathcal{R}$* if there is a record $t$ (for each embedding of $E$ a multiset of $a$ pairs of complex numbers with integral differences, compatible with complex conjugation by swapping the pairs) satisfying two conditions that were not expanded and from which the $c_\tau(\pi)$ are obtained by an operation that was not expanded. If the two conditions are algebraicity and regularity, type $\mathcal{R}$ means regular algebraic. A representation $\sigma$ of $\mathrm{GL}_a(E_u)$, $u$ a finite prime, of the kind used by the project (a record with a space and an action, whose further requirements were not read) is a *local component* of $\pi$ at $u$ if there is a nonzero linear map from the space of $\sigma$ to that of $\pi$ intertwining $\sigma$ with an action of $\mathrm{GL}_a(E_u)$ on $\pi$ (not expanded).

*Galois groups.* For a number field $E$ with algebraic closure $\Omega$, $G_E=\mathrm{Aut}(\Omega/E)$ and $G_E^{ab}$ is its topological abelianisation. A *place of $\Omega$ over a prime $v$ of $E$* is a valuation subring $A$ of $\Omega$ whose maximal ideal meets $\mathcal{O}_E$ in $v$; $D_A$ is its decomposition group and $I_A\subset G_E$ a subgroup attached to $A$ that was not expanded (if it is the inertia group, the statements below read as usual). A *Frobenius element* at $A$ is a $\sigma\in D_A$ with $\sigma x\equiv x^{Nv}$ modulo the maximal ideal of $A$ for all $x\in A$. $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_l$ is an algebraic closure of $\mathbb{Q}_l$ and $\iota$ denotes a field isomorphism $\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_l\cong\mathbb{C}$. A *global Artin map* for $E$ is a homomorphism $\mathrm{art}:\mathbb{A}_E^\times\to G_E^{ab}$ that is an open quotient map, whose kernel is the closure of the subgroup generated by $E^\times$ and a second subgroup (not expanded), such that for every $v$ and $A$ the image of $I_A$ in $G_E^{ab}$ is the set of values of $\mathrm{art}$ on a set of ideles attached to $v$ (not expanded), and which satisfies one further clause that was not read. At a prime $l$, a *tied ring datum* means local period data in ([Local period rings and coefficient comparisons](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.BasicNotions.LocRings/)). A *local identification* at a place $A$ over $u$ means a coefficient comparison for $G_w=D_A$ in that definition, inducing $A$: its embedding $j$ satisfies $A=\{x:|j(x)|_l\le1\}$. An embedding $\tau:E\to\overline{\mathbb Q}_l$ is compatible with a local identification if $\tau=\tau'\circ j|_E$ for some $\mathbb Q_l$-embedding $\tau'$ of its completion field into $\overline{\mathbb Q}_l$. For a homomorphism $r:D_A\to\mathrm{GL}_a(\overline{\mathbb Q}_l)$, use the de Rham, crystalline, potentially crystalline and Hodge–Tate multiset conditions of ([Local period rings and coefficient comparisons](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.BasicNotions.LocRings/)), with finite coefficient models containing the comparison's coefficient field. A *local reciprocity map* at a place $A$ over $u$ is a map from isomorphism classes of irreducible smooth representations of the groups $\mathrm{GL}_n(E_u)$, $n\ge0$, to a set of objects attached to $A$ (not expanded; we call them $\mathcal{W}$-objects) that satisfies a list of laws (not expanded). If $\mathcal{W}$-objects are Weil–Deligne representations and the laws characterise the local Langlands correspondence, it is that correspondence.

*Lattices.* A *lattice* $L\subset\mathbb{C}$ is given by a pair of periods; $g_2,g_3$ and $\wp_L$ are its Weierstrass invariants and function, and $j(L)$ is the $j$-invariant of a Weierstrass curve attached to $L$ by the project (not expanded).

**The statements.**

**(1)** *From type $\mathcal{R}$ to a second property.* For every totally complex number field $E$, every $a\ge1$ and every $\mathcal{K}$-representation $\pi$ of $\mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{A}_E)$ of type $\mathcal{R}$, there is an object $\Xi$ of a type attached to $(E,a)$ such that the pair $(\pi,\Xi)$ satisfies a condition; the type and the condition were not expanded. We say that such a $\pi$ *has property $\mathcal{H}$*. If $\Xi$ ranges over the irreducible algebraic representations and the condition is nonvanishing of relative Lie algebra cohomology of $\pi\otimes\Xi$, this is Clozel's theorem that regular algebraic cuspidal representations are cohomological.

**(2)** Assume the polynomial parametrization in ([Cuspidal characters of finite general linear groups](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/GlobalTheory.greenRegularCharacters_holds/)).

**(3)** *The $l$-adic character of an algebraic Hecke character.* For every number field $E$ with algebraic closure $\Omega$ there is a global Artin map $\mathrm{art}$ such that for every prime $l$, every $\iota$, every Hecke character $\chi$ of $E$ and every family of integers $(n_\varphi)_{\varphi:E\to\mathbb{C}}$ with $\chi_\infty(x)=\prod_\varphi\varphi(x)^{n_\varphi}$ for all totally positive $x\in E^\times$, there is exactly one continuous character $r:G_E\to\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_l^\times$ with $r(\sigma)=\iota^{-1}\bigl(\chi(x)\,m_\infty(x)^{-1}\bigr)\,m_l(x)$ whenever $\mathrm{art}(x)$ is the image of $\sigma$ in $G_E^{ab}$. Here $\chi_\infty$ is a component of $\chi$ and $m_\infty$, $m_l$ are two functions of the idele $x$ built from $n$; none of the three was expanded. If $\chi_\infty$ is the restriction of $\chi$ to $E^\times$ embedded at the infinite places and $m_\infty$, $m_l$ are the products of the powers $n_\varphi$ of the components of $x$ at infinity and at $l$, this is Weil's attachment of an $l$-adic character to an algebraic Hecke character.

**(4)** *Degree-one primes with prescribed Frobenius.* For every number field $F$ with algebraic closure $\Omega$, every finite Galois extension $L/F$ inside $\Omega$, every $g\in\mathrm{Gal}(L/F)$ and every finite set $S$ of primes of $F$, there is a prime $v\notin S$ of residue degree $1$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, a place $A$ of $\Omega$ over $v$ with $I_A\subset\mathrm{Gal}(\Omega/L)$, and a Frobenius element at $A$ whose restriction to $L$ is $g$. If $I_A$ is the inertia group, this is a consequence of the Chebotarev density theorem.

**(5)** *Representations of $G_E$ attached to $\mathcal{K}$-representations of type $\mathcal{R}$.* Let $E$ be a CM field, $l$ a prime, $\iota$ as above, $a\ge1$, $\pi$ a $\mathcal{K}$-representation of $\mathrm{GL}_a(\mathbb{A}_E)$ of type $\mathcal{R}$ and $\chi$ a Hecke character of $E^+$ such that a component of $\chi$ at each real place $v$ (not expanded) takes the value $(-1)^a$ at $-1$ and $\pi$ satisfies a condition relative to a character built from $\chi$ (not expanded). Then there are a homomorphism $r:G_E\to\mathrm{GL}_a(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_l)$, an integer $w$ and multisets of integers $HT_\tau$ ($\tau:E\to\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_l$) with the following properties, which we call *the properties of an attached representation for $(\pi,\chi)$*: $r$ is continuous and satisfies a condition (not expanded); at every prime $u\nmid l$, for every local reciprocity map and every local component $\sigma$ of $\pi$ at $u$, an object attached to $r$ at $u$ stands in a relation to the image of a modification of $\sigma$ under the reciprocity map (object, modification and relation not expanded); $HT_\tau=\{\tfrac{a-1}{2}-z:z\in c_{\iota\circ\tau}(\pi)\}$; $HT_{\tau\circ c}=\{w-h:h\in HT_\tau\}$; and for every tied ring datum and every local identification at a place $A$ over a prime $u$, the restriction of $r$ to $D_A$ is de Rham and has Hodge–Tate multiset $HT_\tau$ at every compatible embedding $\tau$ if for some local component $\sigma$ of $\pi$ at $u$ and some local reciprocity map the image of a modification of $\sigma$ satisfies three conditions (not expanded), and is crystalline if a local component at $u$ satisfies a further condition (not expanded). Under the readings named in the notation, and if the condition on $(\pi,\chi)$ is conjugate self-duality up to the twist by $\chi$, this is the shape of the theorem attaching Galois representations to polarised regular algebraic cuspidal representations over CM fields.

**(6)** Assume the base-change assertion of ([Base change from a definite unitary group](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/UpperTheory.BaseChangeIdentities.baseChangeN/)) in the form in which its local compatibility, sphericality and Satake conclusions hold for the full normalized parabolic product of the $\sigma_{j,w}$ at every finite place.

**(7)** *Existence and uniqueness of certain objects attached to lattices with Galois action.* Let $l$ be a prime, $K$ a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_l$, and $P_0$ an object of a type attached to the ring of integers of $K$, with a ring isomorphism from its underlying ring to a ring $A$ of the project satisfying a condition (type, ring and condition not expanded). (i) For every tied ring datum and every lattice $T$ with an action of the Galois group of $K$, coming from a crystalline representation over a finite extension of $\mathbb{Q}_l$, there are an object $\mathcal{E}$ of a second type and a semilinear map $j$ from a module attached to $(\mathcal{E},P_0)$ to the tensor product of a ring of the project with $T$, such that $(\mathcal{E},j)$ and $T$ satisfy a condition, which we call *realising $T$* (none of this was expanded). (ii) If $(\mathcal{E},j)$ realises $T$ and $(\mathcal{E}',j')$ realises $T'$, every $\mathbb{Z}_l$-linear map $f:T\to T'$ commuting with the two actions comes from exactly one morphism $u:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}'$ with $j'\circ u=(1\otimes f)\circ j$.

**(8)** *Local properties of the $l$-adic character of a Hecke character.* Let $E$, $\mathrm{art}$, $l$, $\iota$, $\chi$, $n$ be as in item (3) and let $\mu$ be a continuous character of $G_E$ satisfying the relation displayed there. For every tied ring datum, every finite prime $u$ of $E$, place $A$ over $u$ and local identification at $A$, the restriction of $\mu$ to $D_A$ is de Rham and potentially crystalline, is crystalline if $\chi$ satisfies a condition at $u$ (not expanded), and has Hodge–Tate multiset $\{-n_{\iota\circ\tau}\}$ at $\tau$ for every $\tau:E\to\overline{\mathbb{Q}}_l$ compatible with the local identification.

**(9)** *A decay estimate for cuspidal automorphic functions.* Let $D$ be a datum, $\theta$ an infinity family, $V$ a Hermitian space, and $e$ a map into $G_V(\mathbb{A})$ satisfying a condition (source of the map and condition not expanded). If $\varphi$ is a cuspidal automorphic function on $G_V(\mathbb{A})$ satisfying two further conditions relative to $e$ and $\theta$ (not expanded), then for every $N$ there is $C$ with $|\varphi(g)|\,h(g)^N\le C$ for all $g$, where $h$ is a nonnegative function on $G_V(\mathbb{A})$ that was not expanded. If the two conditions are smoothness at infinity and uniformly moderate growth and $h$ is a height on $[G_V]$, this is the rapid decay of cusp forms.

**(10)** *Integrability of theta kernels, and an identity with a sum over cosets.* Let $D$ be a datum, $A,B$ Hermitian spaces with $n_A\ge1$, $m$ a Haar measure on $G_B(\mathbb{A})$, and suppose the Witt index $r_B$ of $B$ is $0$ or $n_A+r_B<n_B$. (i) For every $F$-basis of $E$, with $d$ elements, there is a seminorm $\nu$ on a function space attached to the index set of size $n_Bn_Ad$ (not expanded), satisfying a continuity condition (not expanded), with $I(\phi)\le\nu(\phi)$ for all $\phi$ in that space, where $I(\phi)\in[0,\infty]$ is a quantity attached to $B$, $m$ and $\phi$ (not expanded). (ii) For every Weil representation $\omega$ for $(\psi,A^\square,B,1,\chi)$ and every theta function $f$: for all $g$ the function $h\mapsto(\omega(g,h)f)(0)$ descends to an integrable function on $[G_B]$ for the measure induced by $m$ (descent and induced measure not expanded); and if $2n_A<n_B$, then for every Haar measure on $W_{A,B}$ for which a certain subgroup (not expanded) has a fundamental domain of measure $1$, a function $\Phi_f$ on $G_{A^\square}(\mathbb{A})$ built from these data (not expanded) is invariant under a subgroup $Q$, a sum of $\Phi_f$ over cosets attached to a subgroup $P_A'$ in $\Gamma_{A^\square}$ converges at every $g$, and the sum equals the average over $[G_B]$ of $h\mapsto(\omega(g,h)f)(0)$ (the two subgroups, the convergence condition and the coset sum were not expanded). If (i) bounds the integral of the absolute theta series by a continuous Schwartz seminorm and the coset sum in (ii) is the Siegel Eisenstein series, this is Weil's form of the Siegel–Weil formula in the range of absolute convergence.

**(11)** *An identity between a value attached to a family of functions and a mean of theta kernels, for $n_A<n_B\le2n_A$.* Let $D$ be a datum, $\psi$ an additive character of $\mathbb{A}$, and $A,B$ Hermitian spaces with $n_A<n_B\le2n_A$ and $n_A+r_B<n_B$, $r_B$ the Witt index of $B$. Let $\omega$ be a Weil representation for $(\psi,A^\square,B,1,\chi_B)$; let $a$ be adapted to $A$ such that the Iwasawa decomposition holds for $\hat K_a$; let $\theta$ be an infinity family; for each $\tau\in T$ choose complex matrices $s_\tau$, $s'_\tau$ satisfying a condition relative to the Gram matrices of $A^\square$ and $B$ at $\tau$ (not expanded), with $s_\tau$ commuting with the $\tau$-components (not expanded) of the elements of $\hat K_a$; fix a Haar measure on $W_{A,B}$ for which a certain subgroup (not expanded) has a fundamental domain of measure $1$, and a Haar measure $\nu$ on $G_B(\mathbb{A})$; and let $f$ be a function on $W_{A^\square,B}$ satisfying a condition relative to $\psi$, $\theta$ and the matrices $s_\tau,s'_\tau$ (not expanded). Then $h\mapsto(\omega(g,h)f)(0)$ descends to an integrable function on $[G_B]$ for every $g$, and a relation holds between a family of functions on $G_{A^\square}(\mathbb{A})$ built from $a$, $\omega$, the measure and $f$, a complex number $s_0(A,B)$, and a mean of the theta kernel of $f$ over $[G_B]$; the family, the number, the mean and the relation were not expanded. If the relation says that the Eisenstein series of the family has at $s_0(A,B)$ a value equal to the mean, this is a Siegel–Weil formula outside the range of absolute convergence.

**(12)** *Three assertions about a family of functions on $G_{V^\square}(\mathbb{A})$.* Let $D$ be a datum, $V$ a Hermitian space with $n_V>0$, $a$ adapted to $V$ such that the Iwasawa decomposition holds for $\hat K_a$, $\mu$ a continuous character $\mathbb{A}_E^\times\to S^1$ trivial on $E^\times$ and restricting to $\eta^b$ for some $b$, $s_1\in\mathbb{C}$, and $\varphi$ a function on $G_{V^\square}(\mathbb{A})$ in a subspace attached to $(a,\mu,s_1)$, with an attached family $\varphi_s$, $s\in\mathbb{C}$ (subspace and family not expanded). The statement is a conjunction of three parts. (i) The family $\varphi_s$ satisfies a condition (not expanded), and for every $s_0\in\mathbb{C}$ there are $k\ge0$, $r>0$ and functions $H_s$ on $G_{V^\square}(\mathbb{A})$, $|s-s_0|<r$, holomorphic in $s$ and jointly continuous, with $|H_s(g)|\le C\,h(g)^N$ for some $C,N$ and a function $h$ (not expanded), such that $H_s=(s-s_0)^k\mathcal{E}_s$ for $s\ne s_0$, where $\mathcal{E}_s$ is a function attached to the family (not expanded). (ii) The trivially true proposition, which asserts nothing. (iii) For every infinity family, if $V$ satisfies a condition, for every object of a type attached to $V$ satisfying a condition, every Haar measure $m$ on $G_V(\mathbb{A})$ and every pair $f_1,f_2$ in a space of functions on $G_V(\mathbb{A})$ attached to these choices: for $\mathrm{Re}\,s>n_V/2$ a condition with three clauses holds for $(\mu,m,\varphi_s,f_1,f_2)$, and a further condition holds for $(\mu,m,(\varphi_s)_s,f_1,f_2)$; none of these was expanded, and the third of the three clauses is an equality of two complex numbers. If $\mathcal{E}_s$ is the continued Siegel Eisenstein series of $\varphi_s$, part (i) is its meromorphic continuation with moderate growth; if the equality in (iii) is the basic identity of the doubling method, (iii) is that identity and the continuation of the global zeta integral. No local zeta integral occurs in the statement.

**(13)** *An integral over $\mathrm{GL}_n(k)$ equal to a product of local $L$-factors.* Let $k$ have characteristic $0$, let $\mu$ be the Haar measure on $\mathrm{GL}_n(k)$ giving $K_0$ measure $1$, and let $\pi$ be an irreducible representation of $\mathrm{GL}_n(k)$ satisfying a condition (not expanded) and standing in a relation (not expanded) to a representation built from characters with parameters $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n\in\mathbb{C}^\times$ (not expanded).
