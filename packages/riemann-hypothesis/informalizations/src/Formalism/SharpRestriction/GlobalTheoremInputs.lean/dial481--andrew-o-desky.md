## Fixed-data congruence-defect bound

[Theorem GC.0106](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/s/defect.html)

A uniform primewise estimate and a polynomial comparison error imply a uniform eventual bound for the weighted congruence defects. This numerical corollary retains the sum of cohomology lengths, weighted by logarithms of primes, as an explicit term in the bound.

Fix a CM field $E$ of degree $2f$, a nonnegative integer $t$, and $m\in\mathbb N$. For each $a\in\mathbb N$ and rational prime $\ell$, let $S_\ell$ be the set of places of $E$ above $\ell$, let $G_{E,S_\ell}$ be the Galois group of the maximal extension of $E$ unramified outside $S_\ell$, and put $$\mathcal S_{\ell,a}(E)=\ker\!\left[H^1(G_{E,S_\ell},\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell(-a))\longrightarrow\prod_{v\mid\ell}H^1(E_v,\mathbb Q_\ell/\mathbb Z_\ell(-a))\right].$$ Here $E_v$ is the completion at $v$, and $(-a)$ denotes the Tate twist. Let $s_{\ell,a}\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}$ be specified values, equal to $\operatorname{length}_{\mathbb Z_\ell}\mathcal S_{\ell,a}(E)$ whenever this length is finite and $1\le a<t$. Define $V:\mathbb N\to\mathbb R_{\ge0}$ by $$V(n)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\log n\sum_{1\le a<t}s_{n,a},&n\text{ prime},\\0,&n\text{ not prime}.\end{cases}$$

Let $K$ be a number field and $\mathcal V$ a finite set of its finite places. For $v\in\mathcal V$, write $\mathfrak p_v$ for its prime ideal, $p_v$ for its residue characteristic, and $e_v$ for its ramification index over $p_v$. Choose labels $\mu_v$ and $\mu_0$ in a common set. For each $j\in\mathbb N$, choose a nonnegative integer $d_j$ and nonnegative rational values $\eta_j(v)$. Define $$a_v=\frac{\log\operatorname{Norm}(\mathfrak p_v)}{[K:\mathbb Q]}e_v,\qquad \eta_j=\sum_{\substack{v\in\mathcal V\\\mu_v\ne\mu_0}}a_v\eta_j(v).$$ Let $x,L,b,N_c\in\mathbb R$ be the scale parameters and congruence budget. For $j\in\mathbb N$ and $C\in\mathbb R$, the fixed-data congruence condition is the implication $$\sum_{n=0}^\infty V(n)\text{ converges}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \eta_j\le d_j\left[C\{fmbL^2+(\log x)^2N_c\}+\sum_{n=0}^\infty V(n)\right].$$ Consider a family with a set of members $\mathcal F(x)$ for each real $x>x_f$, where $x_f>1$ is fixed. All the fixed data may depend on the member. Fix $\varepsilon>0$ and set $L=x^\varepsilon$, $b=e^L$. Assume $f,m\ge1$, $N_c\ge0$ and $$\sum_{\substack{v\in\mathcal V\\\mu_v\ne\mu_0,\ p_v=\ell}}a_v\le\log\ell\qquad(\ell\text{ prime}).$$ Fix constants $C_1\ge1$ and $C_a>0$, and put $C_N=2000(|C_a|+1)^3$. Assume that there is one threshold $x_*$ such that, for every $x\ge x_*$ with $x>x_f$ and every member for which $\mathcal S_{\ell,a}(E)$ has finite length for all primes $\ell$ and $1\le a<t$, the following choices can be made simultaneously. Choose a positive integer $H$, nonnegative real numbers $\eta'_j(v)$ for $1\le j\le m$ and $v\in\mathcal V$, nonnegative real numbers $\nu_\ell,\Lambda_\ell$ for all primes $\ell$, and $\mathcal N\ge0$. All these choices may depend on the member; $H,\nu_\ell,\Lambda_\ell,\mathcal N$ are independent of $j$.

For a prime $\ell$ and positive integer $A$, write $v_\ell(A)$ for the exponent of $\ell$ in $A$. Require the comparison and polynomial bound $$\eta_j(v)\le\eta'_j(v)+d_jv_{p_v}(H)\qquad(1\le j\le m,\ \mu_v\ne\mu_0).$$ $$\log H\le C_1x^{C_1}L.$$ Put $h_E=|\operatorname{Cl}(E)|$, the class number, $W_\ell=\sum_{1\le a<t}s_{\ell,a}$ and $$\eta'_j=\sum_{\substack{v\in\mathcal V\\\mu_v\ne\mu_0}}a_v\eta'_j(v),\qquad \eta'_{j,\ell}=\sum_{\substack{v\in\mathcal V\\\mu_v\ne\mu_0,\ p_v=\ell}}a_v\eta'_j(v).$$ For every $1\le j\le m$ and every prime $\ell$ occurring among these coefficient places, require $$\eta'_{j,\ell}\le d_j\log\ell\{\nu_\ell+2\ell^{-2}+\Lambda_\ell+W_\ell+v_\ell(h_E)+v_\ell(H)\}.$$ For every finite set $\mathcal Q$ of rational primes, require $$\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal Q}\nu_\ell\log\ell\le\mathcal N,$$ $$6+\mathcal N+\log h_E+\log H+\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal Q}\log\ell\,\Lambda_\ell\le C_NfmbL^2.$$ Under these hypotheses there exist $C,x_0\in\mathbb R$, independent of the member and $j$, such that for every $x\ge x_0$, every member with the stated finite cohomology lengths, and every $1\le j\le m$, the fixed-data congruence condition holds. One may take $C=C_N+C_1$.

## Local lattice, orbit-count and gain conditions

[Definition / hypothesis PHT.D108](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.MainNotions.LinkG_at/)

Fix the full global period datum in ([The convergent doubling integral identity](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/GlobalTheory.T.doubling_basicIdentity_unfolding/)), together with size data, orbit-parameter data and a constant tuple, and real numbers $C,b_0$. Let $K$ be its coefficient number field. For a finite place $w$ write $p_w,e_w,\mathfrak p_w$ for its residue characteristic, ramification index and prime ideal. For each member $u$ of the source's finite index set let $T_u$ be its auxiliary prime, $N_u$ its specified $\mathcal O_K$-submodule, $M_u$ the inverse image of the initial integral lattice under its map into the first chain space, $d_u$ the dimension of its initial complex representation space, and $\ell_{u,w}$ its specified rational length parameter. Write $\tau_w,\iota_w,\gamma_{j,w}$ for the supplied real loss, correction and gain parameters, with $1\le j\le m$.

Use the source's finite abelian group $H$, distinguished subset $T\subset H$, Frobenius step $h_p$, selected set $\mathcal V_p$ of orbit representatives, number $N_p(v)$ of elements of $T$ in the represented orbit, and orbit sum $S_p(v)$. For $t\in T$, let $a_t\in\mathbb Z$ be its supplied weight and $w_{j,t,p,i}$ the supplied partner weight. Put $$B_{j,t}(p)=\sum_{\substack{0\le i<\log_p^{\rm nat}((a_t)_+)+2\\ i\text{ odd}}}\left(\frac{a_t}{p^i}-w_{j,t,p,i}\right)_+,$$ where $(a_t)_+=\max(a_t,0)$ is a natural number and $\log_p^{\rm nat}$ is the integer logarithm, with value $0$ at $0$. Let $T_w\subseteq T$ be the specified subset at $w$.

The local lattice and gain condition is exactly the conjunction of the following five assertions:

1. For every $u,w$ with $p_w\ne T_u$, $$(N_u)_w\subseteq\mathfrak p_w^{\lfloor e_w\tau_w\rfloor_+}(M_u)_w,\qquad \ell_{u,w}\le d_u(\tau_w+\iota_w).$$ If $\mathcal V_{p_w}=\varnothing$, then $\tau_w=\iota_w=\ell_{u,w}=0$.
2. For every $p\in\mathbb N$ and $v\in\mathcal V_p$, $$S_p(v)\le\frac{2mN_p(v)a}{p}.$$
3. If $p=p_w$ is in either of the two prescribed prime classes $\mathcal I,\mathcal S$ and $p\le X''(n)$, then, for every $1\le j\le m$, $$0\le\gamma_{j,w}\le\frac{4|T|a}{p},$$ and $$\sum_{j=1}^m\sum_{t\in T_w}\bigl(B_{j,t}(p)-L_{C,b_0}(p)\bigr)-mF_C(p)\le\sum_{j=1}^m\gamma_{j,w}-\tau_w.$$
4. For every $p$, if the candidate-prime test in the conventions holds and $\Sigma_p\le x^{-\varepsilon}$, every representative passing the orbit test defined below belongs to $\mathcal V_p$.
5. For every candidate prime $p$ and every representative $v$ passing that orbit test, $$\operatorname{Avg}_{\alpha,\beta}\kappa_{p,v}(\alpha,\beta)\le N_p(v)x^{-12}.$$ If also $p<p_0(k)$, the pointwise bound $\kappa_{p,v}(\alpha,\beta)\le N_p(v)x^{-12}$ holds for every pair of integer weight functions on $H$, after restriction to that orbit.

## Admissible points over the members of a family, the row lattice of a point and its maximal slope

[Definition / hypothesis ARAK.D271](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/GlobalTheory.BigTop.FamHyp/)

Several theorems of the proof assert a property of the points over the members of a family, for all members beyond a bound. This page defines the class of families $\mathcal C$, points and admissible points, the phrase "eventually at the admissible points", the row lattice of a point and its maximal slope, the divisibility bound for a family, and the lower bound for the scalars.

**Families.** Datum, size data, family with parameter $\varepsilon$, member, starting scale $x_1$, class of families, and "holds for all sufficiently constrained families of a class" are as in ([Data, families of data, classes of families, and properties of all sufficiently constrained families of a class](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.MainNotions.Closed/)). At a member $(x,i)$ we write $k,m,n,f$ for the numbers of its datum, $K$ for its coefficient field (written $\Lambda$ on that page), $s_K$ for the number of infinite places of $K$, and $b=\exp(x^{\varepsilon})$. Throughout, $\mathcal C$ is the class of the families that satisfy the fourteen standard conditions (N1)–(N4) and (a)–(j) of that page. These are the fourteen conditions on a family in ([Covolume of integral cusp forms](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/GlobalTheory.CovTop.CovolumeBoundOfX/)).

**Points.** Let $(x,i)$ be a member of a family with $x>x_1$. A *point over $(x,i)$* consists of the choices of ([Compatible points for arithmetic covolume bounds](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/GlobalTheory.BigTop.PtAt/)), subject to the conditions of that page, for the arithmetic member $(x,i)$ with its local and theta data and for the height constant $C_{\rm size}$ of the family. A point contains a row-side family in the sense of ([Row-side families of Hermitian spaces](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.L2.Rows.RowSideFamily/)), with its tuple set $\mathcal T$ and its base tuple, written $\mu_0$ here; analytic realisation data; and the coordinate data of the base level and of the sharp level that are computed from the analytic realisation.

**Notation at a point.** For the coordinate data we use the notation of ([Row-side families of Hermitian spaces](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.L2.Rows.RowSideFamily/)) with the following abbreviations. At the base level $b=0$: $d=d_0$ is the common dimension; $X_v=X_{E,0,v}$ is the frame matrix at an infinite place $v$ of $K$; $\mu(w)\in\mathcal T$ is the tuple assigned to a finite place $w$ of $K$; for $t\in\mathcal T$, $P_t=P_{0,t}$ is the projection, $M_t=M_{0,t}$ the integral module and $\iota_t=\iota_{0,t}$ the matrix with values in $K^{d}$; and $\kappa_v=\kappa_{0,v}$. At the sharp level $b=1$: $\lambda_{j,t,\pi,v}=\lambda_{1,j,t,\pi,v}$ for a stage $j$, a tuple $t$, a full label $\pi$ and an infinite place $v$. For real numbers $C$ and $C_H$, the *numerical data of the point with constants $(C,C_H)$* are those of that page; among them are $$h=x^{C_H}\,b,\qquad l_6(j)=l_{6,m}(j)-C\,l_{6,u}.$$

**The row lattice.** For an $\mathcal O_K$-submodule $L$ of $K^{d}$ and a finite place $w$ of $K$ with prime ideal $\mathfrak p_w$, put $$L_{(w)}=\{\,y\in K^{d}\ :\ s\,y\in L\ \text{for some } s\in\mathcal O_K\smallsetminus\mathfrak p_w\,\}.$$ The *row lattice* of a point is the $\mathcal O_K$-submodule $$R_0=\bigcap_{w}\bigl(\iota_{\mu(w)}(P_{\mu(w)}M_{\mu(w)})\bigr)_{(w)}\ \subseteq\ K^{d},$$ where $w$ runs over the finite places of $K$. It is the module $R_0$ of ([Base target slope from determinant and local index bounds](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.L2.Indef.IndefSide.boundsMu/)), formed from the coordinate data of the base level.

**Maximal slope.** For $y\in K^{d}$ and an infinite place $v$ of $K$, let $y_v\in\mathbb C^{d}$ be the image of $y$ under the embedding chosen for $v$, and let $\phi(y)=(X_v^{-1}y_v)_v$, an element of the real Euclidean space $\prod_v\mathbb C^{d}$ with its standard inner product. For a nonzero $\mathcal O_K$-submodule $N$ of $K^{d}$ put $$\sigma(N)=-\frac{\log\operatorname{covol}\phi(N)}{\operatorname{rank}_{\mathbb Z}\phi(N)},$$ where $\operatorname{covol}$ is the square root of the Gram determinant of a $\mathbb Z$-basis. The *maximal slope* of the row lattice is $$\mu_{\max}(R_0)=2\,\sup\{\sigma(N)\ :\ N\subseteq R_0\text{ a nonzero }\mathcal O_K\text{-submodule}\}-\log 2+\frac{\log|\operatorname{disc}K|}{[K:\mathbb Q]} .$$ When $K$ is totally complex and $N$ is a full lattice in a $K$-subspace, the number $2\sigma(N)-\log2+\log|\operatorname{disc}K|/[K:\mathbb Q]$ is the slope $\mu_X(N)$ defined in the conventions of ([Base target slope from determinant and local index bounds](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.L2.Indef.IndefSide.boundsMu/)).

**Admissible points.** A point is *admissible* if its analytic realisation data, with the further choices of the point, satisfy the following seven conditions. The objects named in them are those of the pages cited.

1. Hypothesis (existence of analytic theta maps) of ([Analytic theta maps and primitive row comparisons](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.IndefR.Links.RealDat.ExAt/)).
2. At every stage and at every embedding $K\to\mathbb C$ that is not good, some unit vector of the discrete-series model evaluates to the complex conjugate automorphic form.
3. At every stage there is an action of the finite group on the cusp forms over the field of the original moduli that preserves the integral forms and that, at every embedding $K\to\mathbb C$, corresponds to the complex action multiplied by the designated character.
4. At every stage and at every good embedding $K\to\mathbb C$, evaluation against the chosen extremal vector is injective.
5. At every stage there is a tuple of unit lowest-type vectors of the discrete-series model at the distinguished embedding, each of the prescribed extremal weight, that pairs with a coefficient vector of norm $1$ to the determinant polynomial of ([Analytic theta intertwiners and Weil actions](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.IndefR.Links.RealDat.IsChainW/)).
6. At every stage, Hypothesis (one-map rows) of ([Geometric theta class evaluations](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.IndefR.FWT.OMPins/)).
7. Hypothesis (primitive row comparisons) of ([Analytic theta maps and primitive row comparisons](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.IndefR.Links.RealDat.ExAt/)).

An embedding $\sigma\colon K\to\mathbb C$ is *good* if its restriction to the coefficient field $K_s$ of the original moduli is allowed in the sense of ([One-map and geometric comparison laws](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.IndefR.FWT.FWTie/)), that is, if it sends the designated square root in $K_s$ to the fixed complex square root. Conditions 2 to 5 are four of the conditions of ([Compatibility conditions for covolume points](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/GlobalTheory.BigTop.PtAt.CompatibilityConditions/)), which imposes the seven conditions together with further ones; the objects of these four conditions are those of that page.

**Eventually at the admissible points.** Let $\mathrm{Fm}$ be a family and $Q$ a property of points. We say that *$Q$ holds eventually at the admissible points of $\mathrm{Fm}$* if there is a real number $x_0$ such that $Q$ holds for every admissible point over every member $(x,i)$ of $\mathrm{Fm}$ with $x>x_1$ and $x\ge x_0$.

**The divisibility bound.** A family *satisfies the divisibility bound* if there are real numbers $C$ and $x_0$ such that at every member with $x\ge x_0$ and for every $j\in\{1,\dots,m\}$, $$\sum_{w}\frac{\log\mathrm N\mathfrak p_w}{[K:\mathbb Q]}\,e_w\,\eta_j(w)\ \le\ D_0\cdot C\,x^{\varepsilon}\bigl(f\,m\,b\,x^{\varepsilon}+N_c\bigr),\qquad N_c=\frac{|T_2|\,k\,b\,x^{2\varepsilon}}{n^{2}} .$$ Here $w$ runs over the finite places of $K$ in the finite set $\mathcal S$ of ([Local size and support data](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.BasicNotions.SizeData%E2%82%82/)) to which the datum assigns a tuple different from its base tuple; $e_w$ is the ramification index of $w$ over $\mathbb Q$; $\eta_j(w)$ is the non-negative rational number $\eta_{j,w}$ of ([Complex vector spaces and integral spans](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.BasicNotions.Rows.Data/)), and $D_0$ is the dimension of the space $H_0$ of that page; $T_2$ is the finite set of ([Family fields and Galois data](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.HC.Frame/)).

**The lower bound for the scalars.** This is the following statement. For all sufficiently constrained families $\mathrm{Fm}$ of $\mathcal C$, with the real number $C_H$ of that definition, the following holds eventually at the admissible points of $\mathrm{Fm}$: for every real number $C$, the numerical data of the point with constants $(C,C_H)$ satisfy $$l_6(j)\ \le\ \log\lambda_{j,\mu_0,\pi,v}$$ for every stage $j$, every full label $\pi$ and every infinite place $v$ of $K$.

## Normalized second-moment condition

[Definition / hypothesis AGR.D246](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.MainNotions.LinkS_at/)

Fix $\varepsilon>0$ and $C_\Gamma>0$. For each real scale $x>1$ and each member $i$ of a family at $x$, let $\mathcal U$ be a finite index set, let $H_u$ ($u\in\mathcal U$) be finite-dimensional complex Hilbert spaces, and let $P_u:H_u\to H$ be linear maps into a common complex Hilbert space $H$. Choose positive real normalizations $c_u$, a positive real parameter $D_*$, a nonnegative integer $D_{\mathrm{all}}$, and positive integers $m,f$. All these data may depend on $x,i$; $\varepsilon$ and $C_\Gamma$ are fixed throughout the family. Define $$d_u=\dim_{\mathbb C}H_u,\qquad K_{uu'}=(c_uc_{u'})^{-1/2}P_u^*P_{u'}:H_{u'}\to H_u,$$ $$L=x^\varepsilon,\qquad \Gamma=\exp(C_\Gamma m^5fL).$$ Here $P_u^*$ is the Hilbert-space adjoint, $I_{H_u}$ denotes the identity on $H_u$, and $\|A\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2=\operatorname{tr}(A^*A)$ for a linear map between finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces.

For $C_0\in\mathbb R$, the normalized second-moment condition is the following assertion. There is $x_0>1$ such that, for every $x\ge x_0$ and every member $i$ at scale $x$, there exist real numbers $\eta_0,\eta_1,e_0$ satisfying $$e_0\ge0,\qquad \eta_0\le e^{-x^{\varepsilon/8}},\qquad \eta_1\le e^{-x^{\varepsilon/8}},\qquad \sqrt{e_0}\le e^{-x^{\varepsilon/8}},$$ for which the implication $$\left(C_0^{-1}\le\frac{d_u\Gamma^2}{D_*}\le C_0\quad\hbox{for every }u\in\mathcal U\right) \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \begin{cases} \displaystyle \|K_{uu'}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\le(1+\eta_0)\frac{d_ud_{u'}}{D_*}&(u\ne u'),\\[4pt] \displaystyle \|K_{uu}-I_{H_u}\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\le e_0d_u&(u\in\mathcal U),\\[4pt] D_{\mathrm{all}}\le(1+\eta_1)D_* \end{cases}$$ holds. The errors may depend on $x,i$; the threshold $x_0$ is common to all members.

## Integral maps with height and target bounds

[Definition / hypothesis ARAK.D245](https://andrewodesky.com/rh/lean/Formalism.MainNotions.LinkDIV_at/)

The integral-map condition combines integrality of a family of period maps with bounds for their heights, the target slopes and the finite-place errors. We first define the condition for one arithmetic datum, then its uniform and conditional versions.

Let $K$ be a totally complex number field, with ring of integers $\mathcal O_K$ and a fixed embedding $\iota:K\to\mathbb C$. Let $U$ be the finite nonempty set of source indices. Let $W$ be a finite-dimensional $K$-space and, for $u\in U$, let $V_u$ be a finite-dimensional $K$-space. Give them $\mathcal O_K$-submodules $R\subset W$, $N_u\subset V_u$ and Hermitian metrics at infinity. The dual module and the $K$-linear maps under consideration are $$C=R^\vee=\{f\in W^\vee:f(R)\subset\mathcal O_K\},\qquad B_u:V_u\longrightarrow W^\vee.$$ Write $\Sigma_\infty(K)$ for the finite set of infinite places of $K$. For a metrized $K$-space choose a basis $\mathbf b$ and, at each infinite place $w\in\Sigma_\infty(K)$, a representing embedding $\sigma_w:K\to\mathbb C$ and an invertible frame matrix $X_w$. Here $[z]_{\mathbf b}$ is the coordinate column of $z$ in $\mathbf b$. Its norm is $\|X_w^{-1}\sigma_w([z]_{\mathbf b})\|_2$. Define the real realization $$\rho(z)=\bigl(\Re(X_w^{-1}\sigma_w([z]_{\mathbf b})),\Im(X_w^{-1}\sigma_w([z]_{\mathbf b}))\bigr)_{w\in\Sigma_\infty(K)}.$$ For an $\mathcal O_K$-submodule $A$, put $\Lambda_A=\rho(A)$ and $$\mu_0(A)=-\frac{\log\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda_A)}{\operatorname{rk}_{\mathbb Z}\Lambda_A},\qquad c_K=-\log2+\frac{\log|\operatorname{disc}K|}{[K:\mathbb Q]},$$ $$\mu(A)=2\mu_0(A)+c_K,\qquad \mu_{\max}(A)=2\sup_{0\ne B\subseteq A}\mu_0(B)+c_K.$$ The supremum ranges over $\mathcal O_K$-submodules. Covolume is measured in the real span, is $1$ for the zero module and is assigned $0$ for a nondiscrete module. An infinite integer rank is assigned $0$. Division by zero and $\log0$ have value $0$; a supremum of an empty or unbounded-above set has value $0$. Dual spaces carry dual metrics. For a $K$-linear map $B$ between the chosen metrized spaces, its height is $$h(B)=\frac1{|\Sigma_\infty(K)|}\sum_{w\in\Sigma_\infty(K)}\log\|B\|_w^2,$$ where $\|B\|_w$ is its operator norm.

Fix real height bounds $h_u$, put $d_u=\dim_K\operatorname{span}_K N_u$, and define $$s=\frac{\sum_{u\in U}d_u(\mu(N_u)-h_u)}{\sum_{u\in U}d_u}.$$ A full lattice is a finitely generated $\mathcal O_K$-submodule spanning its ambient $K$-space.

The numerical data consist of positive integers $m,n,k$, real parameters $x,\varepsilon,b,a,\mathcal L,B_0,N_c,\lambda_\Omega$ and $X''$ with $x>1$ and $a>0$, a finite set $T$, integer weights $a_t$ for $t\in T$, source primes $T_u$, distinct auxiliary primes $g_0,g_1$, a distinguished prime $d\equiv7\pmod{16}$ and denominator integers $D_u$. The weights satisfy $$a\le a_t\le a+\frac{a}{kx^{13}},$$ so $a_t>0$. Here $X''$ is the finite-prime cutoff. Let $\mathcal V$ be a finite set of finite places of $K$. For a finite place $v$, let $\mathfrak p_v$ be its prime ideal, $p_v$ its residue characteristic, $e_v$ its ramification index, and $v(\cdot)$ its additive valuation, equal to $1$ on a uniformizer. Put $$\ell_v=\frac{\log\operatorname{Norm}(\mathfrak p_v)}{[K:\mathbb Q]},\qquad [z]_v=2\ell_ve_vz.$$ At every finite place fix real corrections $F_j(v)$, errors $E_{u,j}(v)$ and gains $g_j(v)$, for $1\le j\le m$. Write $$F_0=\sum_{j=1}^m\sum_{v\in\mathcal V}[F_j(v)]_v,\qquad A_0=\sum_{t\in T}a_t,\qquad \phi(y)=y\log y-y,$$ $$M_u=\sum_{t\in T}\left[m\{\phi(a_t)-a_t\lambda_\Omega\}-a_t\log T_u\right].$$

For the complex period comparisons, fix Hermitian spaces $\mathcal H_u,\mathcal H$, embeddings $j_u:V_u\to\mathcal H_u$, $j_R:W\to\mathcal H$ relative to $\iota$, and complex-valued pairings $\mathcal P_u,\mathcal B_u$ on $\mathcal H_u\times\mathcal H$.

For real constants $C_D,C_{\rm abs},C_H$, the integral-map condition is the conjunction of the following nine assertions.

1. $B_u(N_u)\subset C$ for every $u$.
2. $s+\mu_{\max}(R)\le x^{C_H}b$.
3. If $p_v>X''$, $p_v\ne T_u$, $p_v\notin\{g_0,g_1,d\}$ and $p_v\nmid D_u$, then $g_j(v)=E_{u,j}(v)=0$ for every $1\le j\le m$.
4. For $z\in V_u$ and $F\in W$, $$\mathcal P_u(j_uz,j_RF)=\iota((B_uz)(F)).$$ There is a unitary automorphism $V$ of $\mathcal H$ such that for each $u$ there are $c_u\in\mathbb C^\times$ and a unitary automorphism $U_u$ of $\mathcal H_u$ satisfying $$\mathcal P_u(z,F)=c_u\mathcal B_u(U_uz,VF)\qquad(z\in\mathcal H_u,\ F\in\mathcal H).$$
5. $R$ is finitely generated over $\mathcal O_K$ and $\operatorname{span}_K R=W$.
6. $h(B_u)\le h_u$ whenever $B_u\ne0$.
7. For every $u$, $$\mu(C)+h_u-F_0\le M_u+C_D|T|a\mathcal L+C_Dm(B_0+N_c)x^{C_{\rm abs}\varepsilon}.$$
8. For every $u$ and $1\le j\le m$, $$\left|\sum_{v\in\mathcal V}[E_{u,j}(v)]_v-A_0\log(g_0g_1)\right|\le C_DB_0.$$
9. For every $u,u'\in U$, $$\left|(h_u+A_0\log T_u)-(h_{u'}+A_0\log T_{u'})\right|\le C_D\{|T|a\mathcal L+mB_0\}.$$

A family consists of such data indexed by a real scale $x$ and a member $i\in I_x$, with $\varepsilon$ fixed. The uniform assertion, for fixed $C_{\rm abs},C_H$, is $$\exists C_D,x_0\in\mathbb R\quad\forall x\ge x_0\quad\forall i\in I_x:\quad\text{conditions 1--9 hold}.$$

To define the conditional assertion, the family also includes the following local coefficient spaces. For each finite place $v$ and $1\le j\le m$, let $I_{j,v}$ be a finite index set, let $k_\alpha$ be a coefficient field and $M_\alpha$ a finite-dimensional $k_\alpha$-space for $\alpha\in I_{j,v}$, and let $Z_{j,v}$ be a finite-dimensional $K$-space. Mark each finite place as base or nonbase. The covering condition is the eventual equality $$\sum_{\alpha\in I_{j,v}}\dim_{k_\alpha}M_\alpha=\dim_K Z_{j,v}\qquad(1\le j\le m,\ v\text{ nonbase}).$$ Here eventual means for every member at every sufficiently large $x$. Let $\mathcal H^{(0)}$ be an auxiliary finite-dimensional complex Hermitian space and put $D_0=\dim_{\mathbb C}\mathcal H^{(0)}$. For prescribed local numbers $\eta_{j,v}\in\mathbb Q$, put $$\eta_j=\sum_{\substack{v\in\mathcal V\\v\text{ nonbase}}}\ell_ve_v\eta_{j,v}.$$ The defect condition is that there exist $C_N,x_N\in\mathbb R$ such that, for every $x\ge x_N$, every $i\in I_x$ and $1\le j\le m$, $$\eta_j\le D_0C_Nx^\varepsilon(B_0+N_c).$$ The conditional assertion is the implication from the covering and defect conditions to the uniform assertion. It is a condition on a family, rather than a claim that every family satisfies this implication.
